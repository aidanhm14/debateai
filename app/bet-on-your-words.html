Talk is cheap because nothing settles it

A claim in a group chat or a comment thread has no settlement date. Nobody wins, nobody pays, everybody leaves more certain than they arrived. That is why online arguing feels like cardio on a treadmill: effort, no distance.

A debate round is the opposite instrument. It has two sides, a clock, and a verdict. The moment a claim can lose, it starts getting made more carefully.

How the market works

A live round opens on the watch page or on the live board. The motion is the market.

Back a side with play credits before and during the round. Prices move as spectators commit, and swing votes record which speech changed minds.

The round ends and the AI judge writes the ballot: winner, reason for decision, speaker points.

The market settles on that verdict. Your calibration, not a vibe vote, is what gets scored.

Play money, real verdicts

The credits have no cash value. Nothing is deposited, nothing pays out, and credits cannot be cashed out, transferred, or converted into anything. This is not a gambling product and there is no wagering service here.

What is real is the settlement: an actual round, argued by real people, judged on the flow. The stakes are your calibration score and your standing on the leaderboard. That keeps it open to students, which is who this is for.

Prediction is a reasoning drill

Putting a number on your confidence and getting scored against a real outcome is the discipline forecasters drill on purpose. Overconfidence gets priced. Hedging gets priced. After twenty settled markets you know something about yourself that no feed will ever tell you: how often you are actually right.

It transfers to debating too. Choosing what to concede and where to collapse is expected-value reasoning under time pressure. Convert claims into probabilities, then update without ego when the round changes.

The bigger picture

Debatable's thesis is that arguing out loud is the durable skill as machines take over the writing. Markets on rounds are one instrument for that: they make spectating active, they reward changing your mind in public, and because the AI judge settles every round, the system works at any scale, from two strangers at midnight to a whole league. Questions people ask

Can I bet real money on debates?

No. Play credits only: no cash in, no cash out, no conversions, no sweepstakes. Not a gambling product. The stakes are calibration and reputation.

What does "bet on your words" mean?

The house slogan. If you say it, you should be willing to stake something on it. Here the stake is play credits and your standing, and the settlement is a judged round instead of an endless thread.

What settles a market?

The AI judge's ballot on the actual round: a written reason for decision naming a winner. Not an audience poll.

Where do I try it?

Watch a live round or replay, open a live round, or go straight to the markets. Spectating is free.

Can I debate instead of predict?

That is the main event. Get paired with a stranger for a live round, or build a case against the AI first.
