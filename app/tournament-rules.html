Official rules

Debatable prize tournaments

Last updated August 10, 2026. These rules govern every Debatable tournament that offers cash prizes, including The Debatable Open on Saturday, August 29, 2026.

The short version

  • This is a contest of skill. Winners are decided by judged debate rounds, not chance.
  • Entry is free. There is no paid tier and nothing to buy. Everyone competes in one bracket for the same prizes.
  • Cash goes to winners aged 18 and over. Younger winners take the placement and the title; we do not send money to minors.
  • Prizes are fixed and guaranteed by the organizer. They are not a pool of entry fees, so they do not shrink if fewer people enter.
  • The final is streamed live. Entering means you consent to your elimination rounds being broadcast and recorded.

1. Organizer

Tournaments under these rules are run by Debatable (itsdebatable.com). Questions and disputes go to aidandavidhollinger@gmail.com. Competing costs nothing and there is no payment step, so there is nothing to refund.

2. The event

The Debatable Open is a one-day online tournament held Saturday, August 29, 2026, starting 10:00 AM Eastern Time. You compete from your own device on itsdebatable.com. Preliminary rounds run through the day, followed by an elimination bracket. The final streams live on the watch page.

Rounds are one-on-one spoken debates on the platform's standard lay format: constructive and rebuttal speeches on a clock, with the motion announced at the start of the round. Exact round count and break size depend on the number of entries and are posted on the tournament page before round one.

3. Eligibility

  • Competing is open to ages 13 and up, at no cost. Entrants under 18 compete with a parent or guardian's permission.
  • Cash prizes are paid to winners aged 18 and over. A winner under 18 keeps the placement, the title, and the record; the cash for that placement passes to the next highest-placing entrant who is 18 or over. We do not send money to minors, by policy, and no attestation at entry changes that.
  • The organizer, and anyone in the organizer's household, may compete but cannot win cash prizes.
  • One entry per person. Duplicate accounts forfeit all entries.

4. How to enter

Register on the tournaments page with a signed-in account before the entry deadline (30 minutes before round one). One door: free. Same motions, same clock, same judge, same bracket, same prizes for everyone.

You must check in on the tournament page when check-in opens. Registered entrants who never check in are not placed in the draw.

5. Prizes and payouts

  • First place: $500. Second place: $250. Third place: $100.
  • Prizes are fixed amounts funded by the organizer regardless of how many people enter. There are no entry fees, so no part of any prize depends on the number of entries.
  • Cash prizes go to the highest-placing entries in final standings order, subject only to the age rule in section 3.
  • Third place is decided by the tournament's published tiebreak order among semifinal exits (or by a played third-place round if the host schedules one).
  • Payouts are sent within 14 days of the results being confirmed, by a method arranged directly with each winner (bank transfer, PayPal, or similar). A human reviews the final ballots before any money moves; an AI verdict alone never releases funds.
  • Winners may be asked to verify identity and provide tax information where required. Taxes on prizes are the winner's responsibility.

6. What it costs, and how this is paid for

  • Nothing. There is no entry fee, no paid tier, and no card step anywhere in a tournament. Withdrawing costs nothing and there is nothing to refund.
  • If too few entrants check in for a real bracket, the organizer may reschedule the event and will say so on the tournament page. Nobody is out of pocket either way.
  • Prizes come out of the organizer's pocket today. The intended long-term funding is advertising on the site, so that competing stays free permanently rather than free as an introductory offer. Prizes at any given event are only ever what is posted on that event's page.

7. Judging and appeals

  • Rounds are judged by Debatable's AI judge under its published, versioned rubric. How the judge works, its published criteria, and its reliability figures live at the judge integrity page.
  • Every ballot includes a written reason for decision.
  • Either debater may appeal a ballot within the appeal window shown on the round page. Appeals are reviewed by a human, never re-run through a model.
  • Standings become final after the last appeal window closes. After that, results are final.

8. Broadcast and recording

  • Elimination rounds, including the final, may be recorded and streamed publicly on the watch page and connected channels.
  • By entering, you consent to your voice, video, display name, and round content being broadcast, recorded, and replayed as part of tournament coverage.
  • If you cannot appear on a stream, contact the organizer before the event. Preliminary rounds are not broadcast by default.

9. Conduct and fair play

  • During your own speeches, the words must be yours, spoken live. Having another person or an AI feed you lines in real time is grounds for disqualification. Notes and pre-round prep are allowed.
  • Harassment, slurs, or targeted abuse toward any participant means disqualification.
  • Impersonation, account sharing, or manipulating pairings or results forfeits entry and any prize.
  • The organizer's conduct rulings during the event are final.

10. Technical failures

  • If you disconnect mid-round, you have 5 minutes to rejoin before the round is forfeited.
  • If a platform-side failure prevents a round from completing fairly, the host may rerun the round or adjust the schedule.
  • Missing your scheduled round by more than 5 minutes is a forfeit of that round.

11. Changes and cancellation

The organizer may adjust the schedule, round count, or break structure to fit the field, and may postpone or cancel the event for technical or safety reasons. Nobody is charged to enter, so a cancellation costs entrants nothing. Material rule changes before an event are posted on this page and on the tournament page.

12. Legal

  • This is a skill-based competition with no entry fee. Nothing here is a wager, nothing is staked, and outcomes are determined by performance, not chance.
  • To the extent permitted by law, the organizer's total liability to any entrant is limited to the prize amount for the placement that entrant earned, if any.
  • These rules are governed by the laws of the State of Illinois, USA. The Terms of Service and Privacy Policy also apply.
