Official rules

Debatable prize tournaments

Last updated August 19, 2026. These rules govern every Debatable tournament that offers cash prizes, including The Debatable Open on Saturday, August 29, 2026.

The short version

  • This is a contest of skill. Winners are decided by judged debate rounds, not chance.
  • No purchase is necessary to compete. Free entry plays the same bracket with the same judge.
  • Prize entry ($5 for The Debatable Open) makes you eligible for cash prizes and requires you to be 18 or older. The fee is waived automatically for every account created on or before 1:00 PM Eastern on Wednesday, August 19, 2026, and it is waived for anyone else who asks, on identical terms.
  • Free entry cannot win cash. It can win the round, placement title, and public ranking. Any cash attached to that placement moves to the next highest prize-eligible entrant.
  • Prizes are fixed and guaranteed by the organizer. They are not a pool of entry fees, so they do not shrink if fewer people enter.
  • The final is streamed live. Entering means you consent to your elimination rounds being broadcast and recorded.

1. Organizer

Tournaments under these rules are run by Debatable (itsdebatable.com). Questions, disputes, and refund requests go to aidandavidhollinger@gmail.com. Stripe processes payments; Stripe is not a sponsor of any tournament.

2. The event

The Debatable Open runs all day on Saturday, August 29, 2026. Doors open at 10:00 AM Eastern Time and stay open through the day; you arrive when it suits you, get paired, and debate. You compete from your own device on itsdebatable.com. The top of the standings goes to an elimination bracket in the evening, and the final streams live on the watch page.

Three rush hours are published for the day: 12:00 PM, 3:00 PM, and 6:00 PM Eastern Time. These are the moments when entrants are encouraged to be online together so pairing is fastest. They are a convenience, not a requirement, and no entrant is disadvantaged in the standings for competing outside them; rounds played at any hour count the same.

Rounds are one-on-one spoken debates on the platform's standard lay format: constructive and rebuttal speeches on a clock, with the motion announced at the start of the round. You are not required to debate a set number of rounds, and standings use your results across the rounds you do play. The break size and the bracket time are posted on the tournament page on the day.

Every round of the day is decided by the AI panel, except the final, which is decided by the audience watching it. Section 7 has the full rule, including who may vote and the two cases where the panel decides the final instead.

3. Eligibility

  • Prize-eligible entry is 18+. You must be at least 18 years old at the time you enter and confirm this when you enter, whether you pay the fee or have it waived.
  • Free entry is open to ages 13 and up. Minors compete with a parent or guardian's permission and are not eligible for cash prizes.
  • Paid entry is void where prohibited or restricted by law. It is your responsibility to confirm that entering a paid skill contest is lawful where you live. Some jurisdictions restrict paid-entry contests; if yours does, enter free.
  • The organizer, and anyone in the organizer's household, may compete but cannot win cash prizes.
  • One entry per person. Duplicate accounts forfeit all entries and fees.

4. How to enter

Register on the tournaments page with a signed-in account. You can enter before the day or during it, right up until entries close for the bracket. Two doors, one field:

  • Free entry. Same motions, same clock, same judge, same leaderboard. Not eligible for cash prizes.
  • Prize entry, $5. A single payment through Stripe checkout that makes your entry prize-eligible. Pay in any time before entries close, including after you have already played a round.
  • The fee is waived automatically. If your Debatable account was created on or before 1:00 PM Eastern on Wednesday, August 19, 2026, your entry is prize-eligible at no cost. Claim it on the tournaments page and confirm you are 18 or older; the waiver applies on identical terms to a paid entry. Account creation time is read from your sign-in record, not from when you register for the tournament, so you can claim it any time before entries close.
  • The fee is waived for anyone else who asks. Email the organizer before entries close and say you want the entry fee waived. You do not have to explain why, and a waived entry is prize-eligible on identical terms to a paid one. The waiver is what keeps the fee from deciding who can win.
  • Free entry is the other door, and it is not cash-eligible. Entering free without asking for a waiver plays the same field, same motions, same judge and same leaderboard, and can win the placement title, but cannot receive cash.

There is no fixed check-in time. Open the tournament page when you are ready to debate and you go into the pairing pool; leave and come back later and you go back in. Entries close before the elimination bracket is drawn, and that time is posted on the tournament page.

5. Prizes and payouts

  • First place: $500. Second place: $250. Third place: $100.
  • Prizes are fixed amounts guaranteed by the organizer regardless of how many people enter. Entry fees do not set the prize level and no part of any prize depends on the number of entries.
  • Cash prizes go to the highest-placing prize-eligible entries (paid, waived automatically, or waived on request) in final standings order. If a free entrant takes a placement, that placement's title is theirs, and the cash for it goes to the next highest-placing prize-eligible entrant.
  • Third place is decided by the tournament's published tiebreak order among semifinal exits (or by a played third-place round if the host schedules one).
  • Payouts are sent within 14 days of the results being confirmed, by a method arranged directly with each winner (bank transfer, PayPal, or similar). A human reviews the final ballots before any money moves; an AI verdict alone never releases funds.
  • Winners may be asked to verify identity and provide tax information where required. Taxes on prizes are the winner's responsibility.

6. Fees and refunds

  • The entry fee for The Debatable Open is $5, charged through Stripe at pay-in. A waived entry, whether waived automatically or on request, is charged nothing at any point.
  • Full refund if you withdraw 48 hours or more before the scheduled start, and any time the event is canceled or rescheduled by more than 7 days.
  • No refund once you have played a round, and none for disqualification for conduct or for elimination.
  • If fewer than 8 prize-eligible entries have played by the time entries close, the organizer may either run the bracket with prizes as posted or cancel and refund every paid entry in full.

7. Judging and appeals

Every round of the day is judged by the AI panel. The final is decided by the audience. The two are different instruments and the tournament uses each where it belongs: the panel is a consistent, published standard you can prepare against and appeal, and the room is who you were actually trying to convince.

  • Prelims and every elimination round before the final are judged by Debatable's AI panel under its published, versioned rubric. How the panel works, its published criteria, and its reliability figures live at the judge integrity page. Every ballot includes a written reason for decision.
  • The final is decided by a live audience vote. Voting opens when the last speech ends and stays open for three minutes. Whichever side the room votes for wins the tournament and the first-place prize.
  • The panel still judges the final and its ballot is published beside the vote. It does not decide the round. It exists so the final leaves the same written record every other round leaves, and so a debater can see what the published standard made of a round the room called the other way.
  • Who may vote: anyone signed in with a real account (Google or email), one vote each, changeable until voting closes. Guest and anonymous accounts cannot vote. The two debaters in the round cannot vote on it.
  • Two stated limits on the vote, fixed in advance. If fewer than ten votes are cast, or if the two sides finish within two percent of each other, the panel's ballot decides the final instead. Both rules exist so a handful of clicks or a coin-flip margin cannot hand over a cash prize, and both are published here before the event rather than applied after a close result.
  • Either debater may appeal a panel ballot within the appeal window shown on the round page. Appeals are reviewed by a human, never re-run through a model. An audience vote is not appealable, because there is no ballot to review; what is appealable in the final is the panel ballot published alongside it, and an upheld appeal there does not change who the room voted for.
  • Standings become final after the last appeal window closes. After that, results are final.

8. Broadcast and recording

  • Elimination rounds, including the final, may be recorded and streamed publicly on the watch page and connected channels.
  • By entering, you consent to your voice, video, display name, and round content being broadcast, recorded, and replayed as part of tournament coverage.
  • If you cannot appear on a stream, contact the organizer before the event. Day rounds are not broadcast by default.

9. Conduct and fair play

  • During your own speeches, the words must be yours, spoken live. Having another person or an AI feed you lines in real time is grounds for disqualification. Notes and pre-round prep are allowed.
  • Harassment, slurs, or targeted abuse toward any participant means disqualification without refund.
  • Impersonation, account sharing, or manipulating pairings or results forfeits entry, fees, and any prize.
  • The organizer's conduct rulings during the event are final.

10. Technical failures

  • If you disconnect mid-round, you have 5 minutes to rejoin before the round is forfeited.
  • If a platform-side failure prevents a round from completing fairly, the host may rerun the round or adjust the schedule.
  • Missing your scheduled round by more than 5 minutes is a forfeit of that round.

11. Changes and cancellation

The organizer may adjust the schedule, round count, or break structure to fit the field, and may postpone or cancel the event for technical or safety reasons. If the event is canceled, every paid entry is refunded in full. Material rule changes before an event are posted on this page and on the tournament page.

12. Legal

  • This is a skill-based competition. Entry fees are payment for participation in a judged contest, not wagers, and outcomes are determined by performance, not chance. Reaching the final requires winning rounds all day against a published rubric, and the final itself is decided by whether the room found you more convincing, which is the skill the event exists to measure. No outcome at any stage is random.
  • To the extent permitted by law, the organizer's total liability to any entrant is limited to the entry fee paid.
