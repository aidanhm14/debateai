Official rules

Debatable prize tournaments

Last updated August 24, 2026. These rules govern every Debatable tournament that offers cash prizes, including The Debatable Open on Saturday, August 29, 2026.

The short version

  • This is a contest of skill. Winners are decided by judged debate rounds, not chance.
  • Entry is free. There is no entry fee, no paid door, and nothing to waive. No purchase is necessary to enter, to compete, or to win.
  • Two brackets, paired separately. Under 18 and 18 and over run at the same time as separate contests. Entrants are only ever paired within their own bracket, so nobody under 18 debates an adult. Each bracket has its own standings, its own elimination round and its own champion.
  • Cash prizes are 18+. You state your age when you enter, and it decides which bracket you are in. The under 18 bracket competes for the placement title and the public ranking; cash is attached to the 18 and over bracket only.
  • Tips are optional and buy nothing. A $5 tip toward the prize pot is accepted and has no effect on entry, pairing, judging, standings, or prizes.
  • Prizes are fixed and guaranteed by the organizer. They are not a pool of entry fees, so they do not shrink if fewer people enter.
  • The final is streamed live. Entering means you consent to your elimination rounds being broadcast and recorded.

1. Organizer

Tournaments under these rules are run by Debatable (itsdebatable.com). Questions, disputes, and refund requests go to aidandavidhollinger@gmail.com. Stripe processes tips; Stripe is not a sponsor of any tournament.

2. The event

The Debatable Open runs in a single session on Saturday, August 29, 2026. Doors open at 10:00 AM Eastern Time and the first pairings go out then. Preliminary rounds run until 12:30 PM Eastern Time, at which point entries close and the top of each bracket's standings goes to an elimination round; the finals stream live on the watch page at approximately 1:15 PM Eastern Time. You compete from your own device on itsdebatable.com.

The event runs two brackets side by side: under 18, and 18 and over. Pairing happens inside a bracket and never across one, so an entrant under 18 is only ever matched with another entrant under 18. The brackets share the same day, the same motions, the same format and the same judging; they do not share a field, a ladder or a champion.

There is one session and no make-up session. An entrant who is not online at 10:00 AM Eastern Time may still join while preliminary rounds are running, but pairings are made from whoever is online at the time they are made, and a pairing an entrant does not appear for is recorded as a forfeit for that entrant. No entrant is owed a round, a rematch, or an extension for arriving late, for leaving early, or for a connection failure on their own side. The session time is the same for every entrant regardless of where they live.

Rounds are one-on-one spoken debates on the platform's standard lay format: constructive and rebuttal speeches on a clock, with the motion announced at the start of the round. You are not required to debate a set number of rounds, and standings use your results across the rounds you do play within the session. The break size is posted on the tournament page on the day. Times stated here are approximate for the elimination rounds and the final, which begin when the preceding rounds have finished; the 10:00 AM Eastern Time start does not move.

Every round of the day is decided by the AI panel, except the final, which is decided by the audience watching it. Section 7 has the full rule, including who may vote and the two cases where the panel decides the final instead.

3. Eligibility

  • Entry is open to ages 13 and up, at no cost. Entrants under 18 compete with a parent or guardian's permission, and compete only against other entrants under 18.
  • Cash prizes are 18+. You must be at least 18 years old at the time you enter and state this when you enter, which is what places you in the 18 and over bracket. An entrant in the under 18 bracket competes on the same terms in every other respect and cannot receive cash.
  • Your bracket is fixed once you have played a round. Before that you can correct your answer at any time on the tournament page. After it, a change would carry a record into a field it was not earned in, so it is refused; email the organizer if you answered wrongly.
  • Cash prizes are void where prohibited or restricted by law. It is your responsibility to confirm that competing for a cash prize in a skill contest is lawful where you live. Entry itself is free everywhere.
  • The organizer, and anyone in the organizer's household, may compete but cannot win cash prizes.
  • One entry per person. Duplicate accounts forfeit all entries.

4. How to enter

Register on the tournaments page with a signed-in account. You can enter before the day or during it, right up until entries close. One door, two brackets, no cost:

  • Entry is free. Sign in, click once, and you are in. There is no fee, no card, and no waiver to request, because there is nothing to waive.
  • Answer the age question. The entry form asks one question, whether you are 18 or over, with both answers accepted. It decides which bracket you are paired in and whether cash can reach you. It is not a door: both answers enter, free. An entry that has not answered is registered but cannot be paired until it does, and the tournament page asks for it in one tap.
  • A tip is optional and separate. The prize money is funded by the organizer. If you want to contribute, a $5 tip can be paid through Stripe checkout after you enter. A tip is not an entry fee, is not required at any stage, and has no effect on entry, pairing, motions, judging, standings, or prizes. Nobody is asked for one more than once, and no part of the competition treats a tipping entrant differently from any other.

There is no fixed check-in time. Open the tournament page when you are ready to debate and you go into the pairing pool; leave and come back later and you go back in. Entries close before the elimination bracket is drawn, and that time is posted on the tournament page.

5. Prizes and payouts

  • First place: $100. Second place: $50. Third place: $25. The pot scales with the field: if more than 50 entrants register, it goes back up to $850 ($500, $250 and $100), and the new figures are published here before doors open on the day.
  • Prizes are fixed amounts guaranteed by the organizer regardless of how many people enter. Nothing collected from entrants sets the prize level, and no part of any prize depends on the number of entries or on tips received.
  • Cash prizes are decided by the 18 and over bracket's final standings, first, second and third in that bracket. The under 18 bracket runs its own standings and crowns its own champion, with no cash attached to any placement in it.
  • If a placement in the 18 and over bracket cannot be paid (cash prizes are void where prohibited, and the organizer's household cannot win cash), that placement's title and ranking still stand and the cash moves to the next highest-placing entrant in the same bracket who can be paid.
  • Third place is decided by the tournament's published tiebreak order among semifinal exits (or by a played third-place round if the host schedules one).
  • Payouts are sent within 14 days of the results being confirmed, by a method arranged directly with each winner (bank transfer, PayPal, or similar). A human reviews the final ballots before any money moves; an AI verdict alone never releases funds.
  • Winners may be asked to verify identity and age, and to provide tax information where required, before a payout is released. Verification is asked of winners only, at payout, and never of the field: no entrant is asked for identity documents to compete. Taxes on prizes are the winner's responsibility.

6. Cost, tips and refunds

  • There is no entry fee. Entering The Debatable Open costs nothing, so there is nothing to refund on an entry and no charge of any kind is made for competing.
  • Tips are refundable on request. A tip is a voluntary contribution and buys nothing, so if you change your mind, email the organizer before the event ends and it is returned in full. A refunded tip has no effect on your entry, which stands either way.
  • Withdrawing costs nothing and can be done from the tournament page at any time.
  • If fewer than 8 cash-eligible entries have played by the time entries close, the organizer may either run the bracket with prizes as posted, or cancel the bracket and say so on the tournament page. Because nobody paid to enter, a cancellation costs no entrant anything; any tips received are returned in full.

7. Judging and appeals

Every round of the day is judged by the AI panel. The final is decided by the audience. The two are different instruments and the tournament uses each where it belongs: the panel is a consistent, published standard you can prepare against and appeal, and the room is who you were actually trying to convince.

  • Prelims and every elimination round before the final are judged by Debatable's AI panel under its published, versioned rubric. How the panel works, its published criteria, and its reliability figures live at the judge integrity page. Every ballot includes a written reason for decision.
  • The final is decided by a live audience vote. Voting opens when the last speech ends and stays open for three minutes. Whichever side the room votes for wins the tournament and the first-place prize.
  • The panel still judges the final and its ballot is published beside the vote. It does not decide the round. It exists so the final leaves the same written record every other round leaves, and so a debater can see what the published standard made of a round the room called the other way.
  • Who may vote: anyone signed in with a real account (Google or email), one vote each, changeable until voting closes. Guest and anonymous accounts cannot vote. The two debaters in the round cannot vote on it.
  • Two stated limits on the vote, fixed in advance. If fewer than ten votes are cast, or if the two sides finish within two percent of each other, the panel's ballot decides the final instead. Both rules exist so a handful of clicks or a coin-flip margin cannot hand over a cash prize, and both are published here before the event rather than applied after a close result.
  • Either debater may appeal a panel ballot within the appeal window shown on the round page. Appeals are reviewed by a human, never re-run through a model. An audience vote is not appealable, because there is no ballot to review; what is appealable in the final is the panel ballot published alongside it, and an upheld appeal there does not change who the room voted for.
  • Standings become final after the last appeal window closes. After that, results are final.

8. Broadcast and recording

  • Elimination rounds, including the final, may be recorded and streamed publicly on the watch page and connected channels.
  • By entering, you consent to your voice, video, display name, and round content being broadcast, recorded, and replayed as part of tournament coverage.
  • If you cannot appear on a stream, contact the organizer before the event. Day rounds are not broadcast by default.

9. Conduct and fair play

  • During your own speeches, the words must be yours, spoken live. Having another person or an AI feed you lines in real time is grounds for disqualification. Notes and pre-round prep are allowed.
  • Harassment, slurs, or targeted abuse toward any participant means immediate disqualification.
  • Impersonation, account sharing, or manipulating pairings or results forfeits entry and any prize.
  • The organizer's conduct rulings during the event are final.

10. Technical failures

  • If you disconnect mid-round, you have 5 minutes to rejoin before the round is forfeited.
  • If a platform-side failure prevents a round from completing fairly, the host may rerun the round or adjust the schedule.
  • Missing your scheduled round by more than 5 minutes is a forfeit of that round.

11. Changes and cancellation

The organizer may adjust the schedule, round count, or break structure to fit the field, and may postpone or cancel the event for technical or safety reasons. If the event is canceled, nobody is out of pocket, because entry is free; any tips received are returned in full. Material rule changes before an event are posted on this page and on the tournament page.

12. Legal

  • This is a free-entry, skill-based competition. No consideration is required to enter or to win: entry costs nothing, tips are voluntary and buy nothing, and outcomes are determined by performance rather than chance. Reaching the final requires winning rounds through the session against a published rubric, and the final itself is decided by whether the room found you more convincing, which is the skill the event exists to measure. No outcome at any stage is random.
  • To the extent permitted by law, the organizer's total liability to any entrant is limited to the amount that entrant paid, which for an entry is nothing.
