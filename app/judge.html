What gets scored

What the judge is listening for

The criteria are written down before you speak, they are the same for both sides, and you can read them yourself. Five things get a number. The verdict itself comes from the arguments, resolved in a fixed order of force.

Clarity

1 to 10

Structure, signposting, and whether the speech could be followed the first time.

Reasoning

1 to 10

Warrants and link chains. A claim with no mechanism behind it is an assertion, not an argument.

Responsiveness

1 to 10

Clash with what the other side actually said, not with a friendlier version of it.

Weighing

1 to 10

Impact comparison, and whether you told the judge which issue the round turns on.

Persuasion

1 to 10

Whether the case moved a reasonable listener. Concrete stakes, a world you can picture.

Persuasion sits last in the weighing order and can only break a tie the arguments left genuinely level. When it decides anything, the ballot has to say so out loud. Everything above it is comparative work: did the point get weighed against the other side's world, did it survive the response, and did the case ever discharge the burden it took on.

Interruptions and pressure

Getting cut off is not the score. What you did next is.

The judge tracks whether a point survived the response. A later speaker has to beat the answer that was actually given, not repeat the original claim with more confidence. A thread you dropped and never picked back up is a drop, and drops decide rounds.

In a live voice round, a speech that got cut off is marked as interrupted in the transcript the judge reads. So holding the thread under pressure shows up on the ballot as responsiveness instead of vanishing into the crosstalk.

Out of bounds

It does not score how you sound.

Delivery, fluency, accent, speaking speed, volume, polish, and confidence are not scored at all. That fence is deliberate, it is published with the rest of the rubric, and it is enforced in the code rather than trusted to the prompt.

A judge that quietly rewards polish is a judge that marks a second-language debater down for their second language. Speak the way you speak. The ballot is about the argument.

AI debate judge FAQ

Is there an AI that can judge a debate round?

Yes. Hit listen and it hears the round through your device's microphone, then returns a format-native ballot with the result, speaker points, a clear reason for decision, and specific feedback for every speaker. Four-team formats receive a complete 1st-to-4th ranking. You can also paste a transcript if you already have one.

Does this work for Public Forum?

Public Forum is the default format and the case this page was built for. PF runs on lay parent judges who don't flow, so an objective ballot that tracks the summary-to-final-focus chain and the first-versus-second-speaker rules is exactly the gap. LD, Policy, Congress, APDA, BP, Worlds, Asian Parliamentary, and a Quick Clash mode are all supported too.

How accurate is the AI ballot?

It runs the same adjudication engine as our live rounds: format-pinned judging rules, explicit weighing across magnitude, probability, timeframe, and reversibility, and a speaker-point scale calibrated to use the full range. Treat it as a sharp second opinion, not a replacement for a real tournament panel. It catches drops and weighing gaps that a lay judge misses.

Where should I put the phone?

Somewhere it can hear both sides. A table between the two speakers works; so does a laptop at the front of the room. It handles normal debate delivery, including fast speaking, but it cannot judge what it cannot hear, so keep it off the far side of a large room and away from an air vent. It shows you the words as it picks them up, so you can tell within a few seconds whether it is hearing the round.

Do I have to fill anything in?

No. The motion, the format, and who is on which side all get worked out from the round itself. Use the optional setup if you want the motion quoted word for word, need to paste an info slide or other neutral background, or want to pin the format rather than let it infer one.

Does it judge how I sound?

No. Delivery, fluency, accent, speaking speed, volume, and confidence are not scored at all, and that limit is published with the rest of the criteria on the integrity page. What does get scored is clarity, meaning whether the speech could be followed the first time, which is about structure and signposting rather than polish. Persuasion is scored on its own axis, sits last in the weighing order, and can only break a tie the arguments left level.

What happens if I get interrupted or talked over?

Being cut off costs you nothing by itself. The judge scores what happened to the argument: whether the point survived the response, whether you beat the answer that was actually given rather than repeating your original claim, and whether anything you dropped ever came back. In a live voice round a speech that got cut off is marked as interrupted in the transcript the judge reads, so holding the thread under pressure shows up as responsiveness. The same applies to crossfire and points of information.

Does it replace a tournament judge?

No. It gives you an objective ballot when you don't have a flowing judge in the room, and a second read after a round you think you got robbed in. The point is to fill the lay-judge gap, not to stand in for a real panel.

Is it free?

Yes, while Debatable is in beta every tier is $0. Anonymous visitors get a handful of rounds; signing in with Google adds more. The post-beta plans on /pricing are reference only.
