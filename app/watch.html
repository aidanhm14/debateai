Three ways to watch

Live while it happens, after the fact, or just the part that mattered.

Live rounds

Two debaters take opposite sides of one motion and speak against a clock. You sit in on the room read only, so you hear every speech and every point of information as it lands, not an edited version afterwards. Scheduled rounds are listed on live rounds.

Round replays

When both debaters agree to record, the round is captured end to end and published here once it finishes. A replay is the whole thing, opening speech through to the written ballot, not a highlight reel with the reasoning cut out.

Clips

Anyone watching a replay can mark a start and an end, name the moment, and save it. That is how the turn, the dropped argument, or the answer nobody had gets pulled out of a long round and shared as a link.

How to watch a live round

From this page to sitting in the room.
  1. Open the schedule. Upcoming rounds are listed on live rounds with the motion, the format, and the start time.
  2. Take a viewer seat. Rooms hold a limited number of people, so a seat is reserved for you rather than handed out at the door.
  3. Watch read only. You see and hear both debaters. You cannot speak into the round, so nobody gets talked over by the audience.
  4. Read the ballot. When the round ends the judge writes up who won and why, argument by argument. That is the part most debate video leaves out.

If nothing is scheduled when you arrive, the replay library above is the fastest way to see what a round actually looks like. You can also get matched into a round yourself instead of watching.

What you are actually watching

A round is not a comment thread with faces on it.

One motion, two sides

Both debaters get the same motion and are assigned a side. Nobody picks the argument they already believe, which is why the rounds are worth watching. You are seeing people defend a position on the merits.

Timed speeches

Speeches run to a clock, in a fixed order, with the format setting the length and the structure. The clock is the reason a round stays a debate instead of drifting into an argument nobody is refereeing.

A written ballot

Every published round ends with a reason for decision: which arguments landed, which got dropped, and what decided it. Rounds and their ballots are collected in the community feed, and results roll up to the leaderboard.

Questions about watching

Can I watch people debate live online here?

Yes. Rounds between real people are scheduled and run live, and viewers sit in on the room while the speeches happen. When a tournament is being streamed, it plays at the top of this page and on the homepage.

Do I need an account to watch?

A stream playing on this page plays as is. Taking a viewer seat inside a scheduled round means signing in, because each room holds a limited number of people and the seat is held for you.

Are these real people or AI?

The debaters are real people arguing against each other. An AI opponent exists for solo practice when nobody is available, and the judge that writes the ballot is AI, but the rounds published here are people.

What do people debate about?

Motions run across policy, ethics, technology, economics, and international affairs. Both debaters see the motion at the same time and are assigned a side, so neither one arrives with a prepared script.

Can I watch debates that already happened?

Yes, when the debaters agreed to record. Recording is opt in and every participant has to agree before a round is captured, so the replay library only holds rounds the people in them chose to publish.

How long is a round?

It depends on the format. Short matched rounds run a few minutes a side. Full competitive formats run longer, with prepared speeches, points of information, and a reply. The length is listed on each round before you commit to watching it.

Can I take part instead of just watching?

Yes. Get matched with an opponent and you are in a round yourself, with the same clock and the same written ballot at the end.
