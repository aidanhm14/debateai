Ratings and the fine print

Why does a new one sit at 50%?

Each side opens with 100 house points, so a fresh market starts even at 2x. The first real bet moves it.

Points are not your rating

Points are what you put in the pool. Rating is your ladder score. Calling an underdog right moves it more.

Free, virtual, and not cashable

Everyone starts with 1,000. Points cannot be bought, withdrawn, redeemed, converted, or transferred. The winner is decided by a judging panel on a published rubric, not a random draw.

What hot and cold mean

Every market opens even. Hot is the side money has moved toward since then, cold is the side it moved away from. Hot is the crowd's read, not the answer: the cold side is the one paying more if it lands.

Points are a scorekeeping device for reading arguments, with no cash value inside or outside the product. This is not a wagering service and nothing on this page is an offer to gamble. Balances can be reset or adjusted while Predict is in beta.

The pot

The crowd fills it. The debaters win it. The house never touches it.

A prize pot is not a pool of bets. It is funded up front by people who bought something, and it pays out to whoever argued best. The operator takes no cut of it, and structurally cannot: settlement carries no rake, no fee, and no house account, and the build fails if anyone adds one.

The structure

A contest, not a casino.

US law treats something as gambling when three things are true together: a prize, decided partly by chance, that you paid consideration to enter. Remove any one and it stops being a wager. Every surface here removes at least one, on purpose, and this is which one.

The rubric is published before the round, and hashed

Criteria are versioned and fingerprinted, so a ballot stays attributable to the exact standard it was judged under. Nobody can quietly retune the judge after the fact, because a changed rubric changes the hash.

Three judges from three model families

Majority carries, medians set the points, and an even split is never tie-broken. A round nobody can decide voids and returns every stake at face value, because any tie-break rule would be ours.

Appeals go to a person, never back to a model

Either debater can appeal on stated grounds. A reviewer who was in the round is disqualified, the original ballot is kept beside the correction, and a reversal claws payouts back through the ledger rather than just changing the display.

The house earns the same whoever wins

Settlement has no rake, no fee, and no house account, and a build that introduces one fails its tests. We have no position to protect, which is the only reason an operator-run judge can be trusted at all.

What this will never do

  • Take deposits, or let you cash points out
  • Sell points, odds, standing, or a better price
  • Take a cut of a pool, on either side
  • Book a wager against you, at any stake
  • Pay cash to a minor, at any rank
  • Open a prize event before its rules publish

This is the structure being built to, published so it can be read before anyone takes part. It is not legal advice and not a claim that every question here is settled. Prize events open only after specialist counsel signs off on their official rules, eligibility, and age and region requirements; balances can be reset while Predict is in beta. Verify the judging claims yourself at judge integrity, where the live charter, the rubric hash, and the panel agreement figures are served straight from the same code that judges the rounds.
