To practice debating online, open Debatable, choose a question, and take a side. Match with a person in the live queue for a video round, or start a spoken AI round on your own. Each side gets a short opening and a short reply on a shared clock, and an AI judge scores both sides out of 100 and explains the result.

Two ways to practice

Against a person. The live queue matches you with the next person waiting. You agree on one question before the first speech, argue opposite sides on camera, and both read the same decision afterwards. This is the closest thing to a real argument, because the other person has not heard your usual lines and will push on the thing you did not expect.

Against the AI. The spoken AI round is for the times nobody is online, or when you want to run the same question three times in a row. It takes the other side, replies to what you actually said, and stops when you cut in. Use it to warm up, to test a new argument, or to practice keeping your point short.

  • Same round shape either way, so what you learn against the AI carries into a round with a person.
  • The judge scores both the same way, with the rules published before you speak.
  • Every finished round is saved to your account so you can reread the decision later.

What a practice round looks like

A casual one-on-one. Each side opens with a few minutes making its case: two or three points, each one a claim, why it is true, and why it matters. Then each side gets a shorter reply to answer the other's best points and say what is left standing. No jargon, no format to learn, no rulebook. If you can explain a strong opinion to a smart friend, you can do this.

Between rounds, watch how other people do it. The Watch page has real rounds and parliamentary finals, and you can read the judge's decision alongside the video.

How the judge scores you

The judge follows the whole round and writes a decision: who won, a score out of 100 for each side, and the reasons, citing what was said. It rewards answering the other side's actual argument over restating your own, and it says which point decided the round. You can compare the decision with the transcript and contest it. The rules it applies do not change mid-season.

Questions to practice on

Start with something people disagree about at dinner, not a policy paper. The topics library sorts questions by theme, political topics lists what is being argued this week, and Contested gives you both sides of one current disagreement with sources. In a live round, either person can type a question of their own before the first speech.

Practice on a schedule

The live queue is busiest during Clash Hours, 9 PM local in India, London and New York, every day. Turn on the Available pill and the site pings you when someone is waiting for a round, so you do not have to sit and watch the queue.

Debate practice FAQ

How do I practice debating online?

Pick one question, take a side, and argue it against another person or against the AI on a shared clock. On Debatable the round is a casual one-on-one: a short opening from each side, a reply from each side, then a written decision on who won and why. Start at the live queue for a real person, or at the AI round to practice out loud on your own.

Can I practice debate with an AI?

Yes. The AI round is spoken. You take a side, it argues the other one, and you can interrupt it the way you would interrupt a person. It stays on its side, answers what you actually said, and at the end a judge writes a decision with a score for each of you.

How do I find a debate practice partner?

Join the live queue. It matches you with the next person waiting for a round, on video, with one question you both agree on before the first speech. If nobody is waiting, the queue keeps your place and pings you when someone arrives, and Clash Hours run daily at 9 PM in India, London and New York so people show up at the same time.

Do I need to know a debate format?

No. Every round on Debatable is the same simple shape: two people, one question, one side each, a short clock, a decision at the end. There is no format to pick and no rulebook to learn before you start.

How is a practice round scored?

An AI judge follows the whole round and writes a decision: who won, a score out of 100 for each side, and the reasons, citing what was actually said. The scoring rules are published before you speak, and you can compare the decision against the transcript and contest it.

How long is a practice round?

A casual one-on-one runs about fifteen minutes: two speeches of a few minutes each, two shorter replies, and the decision. Spoken AI rounds can be shorter if you want a quick warm-up.

Is debate practice on Debatable free?

The live queue and a limited number of AI rounds are on the Free plan. Current limits are on the pricing page.

Where do I get questions to practice on?

The topics library has current questions by theme, and the political topics index lists what people are arguing about this week. In a live round either person can also type a question of their own before the first speech.
