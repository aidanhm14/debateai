What exists today

A real product before a grand theory.

Debatable is a working voice-first argument trainer. People can match with another person, debate an AI opponent, submit a round for judging, and receive a written decision.

The wedge is competitive debate, where formats, clocks, roles, evidence rules, and judge expectations are specific enough to punish generic output.

The thesis

Writing gets cheaper. Reasoning out loud gets more valuable.

AI can polish written work for almost anyone. It cannot make a person answer a real objection, choose under time pressure, or defend a claim without a script. Those moments are increasingly useful signals for schools, teams, employers, and the people themselves.

01

Practice is fragmented

Finding a partner, a judge, a time, and the right format is still harder than it should be.

02

Feedback is scarce

Most people can practice once. Few can get a useful decision every time and compare progress across rounds.

03

Reasoning has no portable record

Essays have transcripts and grades. Spoken argument rarely produces evidence that can travel with the speaker.

Open questions

The risks are part of the thesis.

Can AI judging earn trust?

Only if decisions are specific, contestable, format-aware, and clearly separated from human tournament authority. The product must show its reasoning, not hide behind a score.

Does the network fill quickly enough?

Live matching is a density problem. Async rounds, scheduled events, AI fallback, and narrow format communities reduce the need for everyone to arrive at once.

Is this only for competitive debaters?

Competitive debate is the wedge because expectations are exact. Courtroom, negotiation, pitch defense, and oral exams test whether the same engine travels.

What should remain private?

Voice, identity, minors, and training data need strict controls. A useful reasoning record cannot come at the cost of participant consent.
