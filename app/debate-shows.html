What the format gets right, and what it leaves out

Surrounded works because it restores stakes. A seat empties when someone gets beaten, the clash is face to face, and there is nowhere to hide behind a thread. Middle Ground works for the opposite reason: it rewards finding the overlap instead of the kill.

What neither measures is movement. Views are not persuasion, and a clip that travels is usually a clip where somebody lost their temper, not one where somebody was convinced. Formal debating solved this a long time ago. The Oxford Union, Intelligence Squared and the Munk Debates all poll the room before and after and report the swing, so the winner is whoever moved the most people, not whoever was loudest.

That is the number this page is built around.

The panel

So: where do you actually stand?

One proposition, ten seconds, and you see where everyone else landed. We ask the same statement again in about ten weeks, which is the only way to find out whose mind genuinely moved.

Answering is optional and takes about ten seconds. You can skip any question.

How the panel works

One proposition at a time

A flat statement, not a loaded question. You place yourself on a seven-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree, and separately say how sure you are. Direction and certainty are different things and we keep them apart.

Asked again later

The same wording comes back after about ten weeks. We do not show you your old answer, because seeing it would anchor the new one. The gap between the two is the measurement.

Sometimes after a round

Finish a debate and you may get a proposition tied to what you just argued, with room to say what changed your mind. An opinion with a stated cause behind it is worth more than a tally.

Where the room stands

Live splits across the propositions with enough answers to be worth showing. Anything thinner than that is left off rather than dressed up as a finding.

Questions people ask

What is Jubilee's Surrounded?

A debate series from Jubilee Media where one person sits in the middle of a circle and argues against a group of 20 to 25 people who disagree with them. Opponents take the facing seat one at a time, and either side can leave the chair. Episodes with Mehdi Hasan, Sam Seder and Charlie Kirk each drew millions of views.

What is Middle Ground on Jubilee?

The companion format. Two opposed groups sit facing each other and answer prompts by stepping forward when they agree. It is built to find overlap rather than to produce a winner, which makes it the cooperative counterpart to Surrounded's adversarial setup.

Are there other debate shows like Surrounded?

The Oxford Union publishes full formal debates on YouTube, where speakers argue a fixed motion and the audience votes. Intelligence Squared and the Munk Debates run the same before-and-after audience vote. The shared idea across all of them is that the score is how many minds moved, not who talked loudest.

How can I practice the Surrounded style of debate?

Run a timed round against an AI opponent that argues back and interrupts, or get matched against a real person for a live round. An AI judge writes a ballot at the end explaining the decision.
