The panel

What people who argue for a living actually think.

Most polls ask strangers what they believe once, then report the number as if belief holds still. This one asks people who spend their weekends arguing both sides of hard questions, and then asks them again months later. The interesting number is not where the room lands. It is who moved.

Answering is optional and takes about ten seconds. You can skip any question.

How it works

One proposition at a time

A flat statement, not a loaded question. You place yourself on a seven-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree, and separately say how sure you are. Direction and certainty are different things and we keep them apart.

Asked again later

The same wording comes back after about ten weeks. We do not show you your old answer, because seeing it would anchor the new one. The gap between the two is the measurement.

Sometimes after a round

Finish a debate and you may get a proposition tied to what you just argued, with room to say what changed your mind. An opinion with a stated cause behind it is worth more than a tally.

Where the room stands

Live splits across the propositions with enough answers to be worth showing. Anything thinner than that is left off rather than dressed up as a finding.

What we do with it

Answers are stored against a pseudonymous panel id, never your account id. They power the splits on this page for everyone.

We also license an anonymized slice to research organizations, under exactly the same opt-in that covers debate rounds: off by default, 18 or over only, future answers only, and never for signed-out visitors. If you have not switched that on in your profile, your answers stay here and go nowhere else. The full terms are in the privacy policy, and the corpus terms sit alongside them at the research page.

Turn licensing on or off any time in profile settings. Ask us to delete your answers and we will, including from future shipments.
