Pick the judge before the judge picks the ballot.

Two honest judges can hear the same round and disagree. One follows every technical drop. Another cares most about what a reasonable listener would believe. Choose the audience you want to practice for.

Clear and persuasive

Lay judge

Rewards explanations a smart newcomer can follow. Jargon gets no credit by itself, and a missed technical point does not automatically end the round.

Use it for: parent judges, public audiences, classrooms, and early PF.
Choose this judge
Tracks every response

Flow judge

Follows each argument through the round. Warranted extensions and important drops matter when a debater explains their ballot impact.

Use it for: most competitive tournament practice.
Choose this judge
Strict line by line

Technical judge

Tech can outweigh the judge's prior view of truth. A plausible, warranted, extended, and unanswered argument can decide the ballot.

Use it for: national-circuit Policy or LD and fast, technical PF.
Choose this judge
Content and delivery

Communication

Clarity, structure, responsiveness, and delivery can break a close tie. Presentation cannot rescue a case with no surviving offense.

Use it for: Worlds, Asian Parliamentary, Congress, and public-facing rounds.

Choose a decision paradigm

A paradigm is the judge's decision rule. These presets summarize patterns that recur in public judge philosophies.

Format default

Choose for me

Uses the decision rule normally expected for the selected or detected format, then names it in the ballot.

Best for: a first ballot, or a round where you do not know the judge.
Use this paradigm
Debaters set the rules

Tabula rasa

The judge starts from a blank slate. The debaters establish the framework, burdens, and voting issues. The judge minimizes intervention.

Best for: Policy, circuit LD, and rounds with clear judge instruction.
Use this paradigm
Net benefits

Policymaker

Compares the proposal with the status quo and any counterproposal. The winner offers the best warranted balance of benefits, costs, and solvency.

Best for: plans, disadvantages, counterplans, and implementation debates.
Use this paradigm
The proposal carries each burden

Stock issues

Tests topicality, harms or significance, inherency, and solvency as distinct burdens. A missing burden can defeat the proposal.

Best for: traditional Policy, classroom debate, and novice rounds.
Use this paradigm
Tech over truth

Games player

Treats debate as a technical competitive game. Dropped, warranted arguments are concessions, even when the judge personally doubts the claim.

Best for: fast circuit rounds with strict line-by-line debating.
Use this paradigm
Truth over tech

Truth seeker

Prefers credible evidence, sound warrants, and real-world plausibility over tricks or claims that survive only because they were missed.

Best for: lay PF, traditional LD, public debates, and mixed audiences.
Use this paradigm
Test the whole motion

Hypothesis tester

Treats the motion as a general claim and tests it across the most important likely cases, not only one narrow model or example.

Best for: Parliamentary motions and general propositions.
Use this paradigm
The room matters

Communication

Evaluates substance alongside clarity, structure, responsiveness, and audience adaptation. Delivery may settle a close round.

Best for: Worlds, Asian Parliamentary, Congress, and classroom debate.
Use this paradigm
