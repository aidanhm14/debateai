The day

What a tournament day actually looks like.

Start to finish it is an afternoon, not a weekend. You can lose a round and keep debating; the preliminary rounds all count toward whether you break.

  1. 30 min before

    Check in

    Open this page, press check in, and you are in the draw. Miss it and you sit out round one, so set a reminder.

  2. Round one

    You get a motion, a side, and an opponent

    The pairing is done for you. You get a few minutes to think, then you speak. Your opponent is another person, not a bot.

  3. After every round

    A written verdict, not just a score

    The judge names who won, which arguments survived, and what you should have answered. That ballot is yours whether you won or lost.

  4. Through the day

    Preliminary rounds

    Everyone debates several rounds regardless of record, so one bad draw does not end your day. Wins and speaker scores decide the standings.

  5. Late afternoon

    The break, then the bracket

    The top of the standings advances to elimination rounds. From there it is single elimination: win and you keep going.

  6. The end

    The final, streamed live

    Two people left, one motion, everyone watching. It goes out on the watch page for anyone who wants to see it.

Exact round count and break size depend on how many people enter, and both are posted before round one. A small field runs shorter.

Before you talk yourself out of it

You need less than you think.

Most people who enter have never been in a competitive round. The format is written in plain English and the judge explains itself.

💻

A laptop and a mic

Headphones help. Everything runs in the browser, so there is nothing to install.

🗣️

No experience

No jargon, no debate-club vocabulary, no prepared case. You make a point, back it up, and answer theirs.

🕐

An afternoon

Rounds are short. You are not reading for three hours; you are talking for a few minutes at a time.

🌍

Any country

It is online, so the field is global. Times are posted in Eastern; your calendar will convert them.

Entry Free

One seat, no cost, the same bracket as everyone else. You get a written verdict on every round you debate, and your scores post to the public leaderboard. Cash prizes go to the highest-placing entrants aged 18 or over.

No purchase is necessary to compete or to climb the leaderboard. Cash prizes are paid to winners aged 18 or over, and are void where prohibited. The official rules carry eligibility, the payout ladder, and streaming consent. A human confirms the final ballots before any money moves.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Anything not covered here lives in the official rules, which are worth two minutes before you pay anything.

I have never debated. Am I going to embarrass myself?

You will lose some rounds. Everyone does, including people who have done this for years. The default format is written for people who have never competed: no jargon, no prepared case, no citations to memorise. You make a point, explain why it holds, and answer what the other person said. The judge writes its reasoning in plain English, so a round you lose still tells you exactly what to fix.

There is prize money. Where does it come from?

Prizes are funded by us, not raised from a pot of entry fees. That is why the amounts are fixed: they do not grow if a thousand people enter and they do not shrink if twelve do. The one condition on the money is age. Cash prizes are paid to winners aged 18 or over, and anyone younger competes for the placement and the ranking rather than the cash.

Who judges, and is that actually fair?

Rounds are judged by an AI judge working from a rubric that is published before the round, not written after it. It gives a written reason for every decision, and either debater can appeal, at which point a human reviews it. The criteria, the reliability figures, and the appeal process are all public at the judge integrity page. Nobody has to take our word for how a round was decided.

What if I enter and then cannot make it?

Withdraw from this page and nothing happens to you; there is no fee to lose. The one thing worth doing is withdrawing rather than silently not turning up, because the draw is built around who is checked in and a no-show hands somebody a round they did not debate.

Will I be on a stream?

Only if you make it deep into the bracket. Preliminary rounds are not broadcast. Elimination rounds, including the final, may be streamed and recorded, and entering counts as agreeing to that. If being on camera is a problem, say so before the event and it can be worked around.

Can I just watch?

Yes, and it costs nothing. Live rounds and past ones are on the watch page, no account required. Watching a couple of rounds is the cheapest way to decide whether you want to enter the next one.

How big is the field?

These are early events, so expect a bracket you can actually win rather than a thousand-person tournament. That cuts both ways and we would rather say it than imply otherwise. The number of entries is shown on this page as they come in.
