The day

Turn up whenever. Debate. Leave.

There is no start time you have to make. Doors open in the morning and stay open all day, so you drop in when it suits you, get paired with whoever else is around, and play as many rounds as you feel like.

  1. Any time

    Show up

    Open this page when you are ready. No check-in window to miss, no reminder to set. If you are around at noon, you play at noon.

  2. A minute later

    You get a motion, a side, and an opponent

    The pairing is done for you. A few minutes to think, then you speak. Your opponent is another person, not a bot.

  3. After every round

    A written verdict, not just a score

    The judge names who won, which arguments survived, and what you should have answered. That ballot is yours whether you won or lost.

  4. As often as you like

    Play again, or stop

    Go again straight away, come back after dinner, or call it a day. Your results across the rounds you play are what set your standing, so more rounds is more chances, not an obligation.

  5. Evening

    Entries close, the bracket is drawn

    The top of the standings goes into single elimination. Win and you keep going. You need to be around for this part, and the time is posted on the day.

  6. The end

    The final, and the room decides it

    Two people left, one motion, everyone watching on the watch page. Every round before this one is called by the AI judge. This one is called by the audience: when the last speech ends, anyone signed in and watching votes, and the side the room picks wins the tournament. The AI judge still writes its ballot on the final and it is published next to the vote, so you can see what the rubric made of a round the room may have called the other way.

Break size and the exact bracket time depend on how many people play, and both are posted on this page during the day. Playing one round still puts you on the board.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Anything not covered here lives in the official rules, which are worth two minutes before you pay anything.

I have never debated. Am I going to embarrass myself?

You will lose some rounds. Everyone does, including people who have done this for years. The default format is written for people who have never competed: no jargon, no prepared case, no citations to memorise. You make a point, explain why it holds, and answer what the other person said. The judge writes its reasoning in plain English, so a round you lose still tells you exactly what to fix.

There is prize money. Where does it come from?

Prizes are funded by us, not raised from a pot of entry fees. Cash eligibility needs a prize entry and age 18 or older, and the fee is waived for every account created before Friday, August 22; after that, ask and it is waived. Free entrants play the same field and can win the placement title and ranking, but cannot receive cash. That cash moves to the next highest paid entrant.

Who judges, and is that actually fair?

Rounds are judged by an AI judge working from a rubric that is published before the round, not written after it. It gives a written reason for every decision, and either debater can appeal, at which point a human reviews it. The criteria, the reliability figures, and the appeal process are all public at the judge integrity page. Nobody has to take our word for how a round was decided.

The final is the one exception, and it is deliberate. It is decided by the people watching: voting opens when the last speech ends, anyone signed in with a real account gets one vote, and the side the room picks wins. The AI judge still writes its ballot on the final and it is published beside the vote. Prelims reward the argument that survives a published standard; the final rewards the one that actually moved a room, and you should know which you are walking into. The official rules carry the full rule, including the two cases where the panel decides the final instead.

What if I enter and then cannot make it?

Nothing happens. There is no set start you can miss, so an entry you never use costs you nothing and costs nobody else a round. If you paid in and the day falls through entirely, ask before entries close and you get the fee back.

Will I be on a stream?

Only if you make it deep into the bracket. Preliminary rounds are not broadcast. Elimination rounds, including the final, may be streamed and recorded, and entering counts as agreeing to that. If being on camera is a problem, say so before the event and it can be worked around.

Can I just watch?

Yes, and it costs nothing. Live rounds and past ones are on the watch page, no account required. Watching a couple of rounds is the cheapest way to decide whether you want to enter the next one.

How big is the field?

These are early events, so expect a bracket you can actually win rather than a thousand-person tournament. That cuts both ways and we would rather say it than imply otherwise. The number of entries is shown on this page as they come in.
