02 · SCHEMA

Nine records carry the event.

Documents are small, explicit, and versioned. Derived standings never overwrite the inputs that produced them.

tournaments/{id}

Owner, timezone, format policy, status, ledger version.

divisions/{id}

Format, eligibility, round count, break rule, judging mode.

entries/{id}

Members, institution, eligibility, check-in, side history.

rounds/{id}

Sequence, motion, preview run, committed version, release state.

pairings/{id}

Teams, sides, judge, room, constraint report, override reason.

room_sessions/{id}

Daily room, scoped participants, token expiry, timing state.

ballots/{id}

Official judge, scores, RFD, signature, lock, release status.

standings_snapshots/{id}

Immutable ranking inputs, tiebreak order, published version.

audit_events/{id}

Actor, action, reason, before and after versions, timestamp.

05 · SECURITY

Private by default. Audited when humans intervene.

Tournament roles are narrower than account roles. A judge can write one assigned ballot, not read private registration data or publish a round.

Server-only mutations

Firebase identity and App Check are verified in Netlify. Firestore clients never commit pairings, locks, or releases directly.

Tokenized Daily rooms

Room-scoped, role-scoped tokens expire after the round. Public room names and reusable meeting tokens are rejected.

Append-only audit

Pairing overrides, judge changes, ballot unlock exceptions, and releases record actor, reason, and ledger version.

Ballot isolation

Drafts are visible only to the assigned judge and authorized directors. Competitors see ballots after release.

AI policy boundary

Organizer events call shadow AI only after the human ballot lock. Model output cannot enter the official write path.

Data minimization

Public standings expose competitive records. Contact details, eligibility evidence, and room tokens stay private.

08 · FAQ

Policy before product.

Judging authority, pairing rules, privacy, and overrides are declared before the first registration.

Does Debatable replace human judges?

Organizer-hosted events default to human-official ballots. Shadow AI can run only after the human ballot is locked. Debatable Cups may choose AI-official judging and disclose that policy before registration.

How are pairings produced?

The server receives a fixed input snapshot, tournament policy, and seed. It produces the same preview for the same inputs. Hard and soft constraint results are visible before commit. Overrides require a reason and remain in the audit log.

Why start with a two-sided format?

It tests side balance, rematch prevention, power pairing, judge assignment, room state, ballots, and elimination advancement without adding multi-team ranking logic to the first live event.

What existing infrastructure is reused?

The design reuses Debatable's current tournaments route, live-round experience, Firebase and Firestore stack, Netlify functions, Daily video rooms, and adjudication system. The new work is the authoritative tournament ledger and the role-specific views around it.
