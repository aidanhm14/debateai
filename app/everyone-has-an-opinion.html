The feed rewards the take, not the argument

Social platforms pay for heat: the sharper the take, the wider it travels, and nobody ever loses an argument in their own comment section. You can go years holding opinions that have never once been pushed back on by someone with equal time and a reason to win.

A round is a different economy. Equal speaking time, a stranger with the opposite brief, and a judge who writes down who actually carried the argument. Heat stops working. Warrants start.

What the judge gives you

Every round ends with a written ballot: the winner, the reason for decision, speaker points, and one drill to run before your next round. Losing well is the fastest way to find the weak warrant you have been carrying around.

From hot take to case

An opinion becomes an argument when it grows a warrant and an impact. The claim, warrant, impact guide covers the anatomy; weighing covers why judges pick one true thing over another true thing; rebuttal covers taking apart the other side without dodging it. Ten minutes of reading, then back to the room.

The bigger picture

The bet behind Debatable: as machines take over the writing, the human skill that holds its value is reasoning out loud under pressure. Feeds train the opposite. So we build rooms where opinions get tested instead of amplified, with an AI judge in every one of them, which means the format scales anywhere: two strangers at midnight, a classroom in any country, a whole league. Questions people ask

Where can I argue with people online?

Debatable's matchmaker pairs you with a random person for a live video debate: motion, sides, clock, ballot. Prefer not to go live? Record an async round and a human answers later. Free while in beta.

Is there an app that debates your opinions?

Yes. The AI opponent argues the other side of any claim you bring, by voice or text, and it disagrees intelligently instead of nodding along. When you want a human, the matchmaker pairs you and the AI moves to the judge's chair.

What if my opinion is unpopular?

Unpopular is fine; undefended is the problem. A round gives an unpopular view the fairest hearing it will ever get: equal time, a neutral judge, a written reason for decision. You find out it is stronger than the room thought, or exactly where it breaks.

Do I have to be a competitive debater?

No. Quick Clash rounds are built for first-timers: short speeches, plain rules, a judge that explains itself in plain language. The competitive formats are there when you want them.

Is it free?

Free while in beta. Start as a guest; signing in unlocks more rounds and your history.
