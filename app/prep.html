The prep loop

Four steps. The whole thing fits in a lunch break.

1

Find the clash

Write the one sentence both sides actually disagree about. A topic is not yet an argument.

2

Build the case

Keep three reasons. For each one, state why it is true and why the consequence matters.

3

Attack it yourself

Write the strongest reply the other side could give. If you cannot answer it, change the case now.

4

Rehearse the opening

Say the first thirty seconds out loud. Cut every sentence that delays the first real claim.

The hour before the round

Prep time collapses under pressure. This is the order that survives it, working backwards from the clock.

  1. 60 minutes out Read the motion twice, then close it.

    Say the clash out loud in one sentence. If you cannot, you have not understood the motion yet, and no amount of research fixes that.

  2. 45 minutes out Write three arguments. Not five.

    Five arguments means five shallow ones, and the shallow ones are where you lose. Three with real warrants beats five with tags.

  3. 30 minutes out Argue the other side out loud.

    The single highest-value prep move, and the one nobody does alone. Whatever you cannot answer here is what the opposition opens with.

  4. 15 minutes out Cut your worst argument.

    You already know which one it is. Carrying it costs you time in every speech and gives the other bench a free win on the flow.

  5. 5 minutes out Rehearse the first thirty seconds.

    Openings are the only part you can fully control. Land it clean and the rest of the speech settles.

Pressure-testing is easier when something pushes back. Review direct rebuttal, then test the case against the AI.

A case you can actually use

The Learn page teaches six moves. Prep is where you apply them to the question in front of you. Check the case against each one before you start the round.

Complete argumentClaim, reason, consequence. Miss one and the point collapses. Clear comparisonCompare size, likelihood, timing, and whether the harm can be undone. Strongest replyAnswer the best objection, not the easiest version of it. Trackable structureNumber the reasons and make every transition easy to follow. Useful questionsExpose assumptions and missing links before the next speech. Plain deliveryStart with substance. Cut throat-clearing and borrowed jargon.

What prep usually misses

Prep alone rewards fluency, not accuracy. Rehearsing a case by yourself makes you smoother at saying it. It does nothing about whether the warrant holds, because nothing in the room is testing it.

The test is an opponent who takes the strongest version of the other side and goes after your weakest link. That is what a practice round is for, and it is the thing that is hardest to schedule late at night when you need it.

Questions

How do I prepare for a debate in under an hour?

Work backwards from the clock. Read the motion and state the clash in one sentence, write three arguments rather than five, argue the opposing side out loud, cut your weakest argument, then rehearse your first thirty seconds. The full breakdown is in the hour-before section above.

Can I practice debate without a partner?

Yes. A voice round against the AI runs on a clock, answers what you actually said, and gives you a written decision at the end. Use it to test the case after you finish the prep plan.

Is this free?

An anonymous visitor gets one AI round, and signing in brings the typed allowance to ten rounds. Live video requires Google sign-in. Free and paid plans are listed on the pricing page.

What if I do not know debate jargon?

You do not need it. Start with a claim, explain why it is true, show why the consequence matters, then answer the strongest reply. The Learn page teaches each move in plain language.

What does the judge's decision contain?

A winner, a written explanation of what decided the round, scores for both sides, and one concrete thing to improve before the next one.

Can my team or club use this for practice?

Yes. Coaches use it to fill the gap between scheduled practices, particularly for squads without enough people to run a full round. Details for programs are on the schools page.
