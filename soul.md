## 1. What this is (one sentence)

Debatable is a **voice-first adversarial-argument trainer** built for competitive debaters, extending to any professional whose job is arguing out loud. Lives at **itsdebatable.com** (canonical since 2026-07-22; debateai.com and the other legacy domains 301 there).

**Brand system:** the product name is **"Debatable"**, hosted at **itsdebatable.com**. It is the only public name across visible copy, metadata, structured data, generated audio, native labels, and extensions. CTAs describe the action instead of acting as a second brand. The canonical full-round surface is `/practice`; retired public URLs only survive as server-side redirects.

Not a chatbot. Not a writing assistant. Not a research tool. A **live sparring partner** that gives you real speeches against a clock, then grades you like a judge would.

## 2. Who it's for

**Primary (where the product is sharpest):**
- Competitive debaters, HS through college
- Format-accurate across APDA, BP, Policy, LD, PF, Worlds (WUDC), Asian Parli, Congress, MUN, and Quick Clash
- Audience is **global** competitive debaters. The "~80% Indian" traffic figure (Apr 2026) was an ad-driven anonymous spike, not a durable base — see §8. Don't hard-code copy to one geography. Lead with the formats themselves: **Asian Parli, WSDC, and BP** are the highest-volume school circuits worldwide and APDA / Policy / LD / PF anchor the US college and HS scenes, so a format-first hero reads right everywhere. Direction is a broad global-community framing, not an India-targeted one.

**Secondary (same engine, different room):**
- Lawyers. opening / closing / cross-ex / motion practice
- Sales. objection drills, demo rehearsal, negotiation
- Teams. red-team, pre-mortem, stakeholder rehearsal
- Founders. VC pitch rehearsal, board prep, hard conversations
- Students. interview prep, admissions essays, thesis defense
- Speakers. debate prep, media training, panel rehearsal

Each gets its own landing page under `/{slug}` with audience-tuned copy and a distinct color identity.

**NOT for:**
- People looking for a generic AI chat buddy. The whole point is that it **pushes back**. it's not a cheerleader.
- People who want written essays. The voice round is the moat.
- Casual learners who don't care about format accuracy. This is for people who debate seriously enough to care that APDA doesn't use tagged cards and Policy does.

## 3. The wedge (what makes it different)

Five things, in order of importance:

1. **Format accuracy.** Policy gets tagged-card delivery. LD gets value/criterion. BP gets extensions + whip structure. APDA is impromptu with no fake citations. No other AI-debate tool does this. they all speak in generic "Harvard debate society" English. This is the hardest moat to copy because it requires actually knowing debate.

2. **Voice round + judge RFD.** You can run a timed speech, hear pushback, take POIs, and get a judge ballot. That's the moat against ChatGPT. voice + timer + format-specific structure.

3. **Built by someone who won.** The creator is a national APDA champion. Most AI-product founders can say "I used AI to build this"; very few can say "I actually argued in this format at the top of the country." Surface this on the landing, not just the footer.

4. **Six brains + HD voice on paid.** Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Open Lab. DeepSeek (added 2026-05-15) routes direct to the DeepSeek API and pins to `deepseek-chat`; Open Lab (added same day) routes through OpenRouter and defaults to Nous Hermes 4 405B with an env-overrideable pool that also covers Mistral Large, Qwen3-235B, and Llama 4 Maverick/Scout. The $10/year Individual tier is where users get the full engine. BYOK is Anthropic-only and labeled as such. (Currently in beta — every tier is $0 while we figure out pricing; see §7.)

5. **A learning loop wired to sharpen from rated rounds** (capture live since 2026-05-13; the nightly distillation has NEVER produced an artifact, see the 2026-08-11 decision-log entry, so do not claim it externally). Every typed AI turn and every voice-round transcript writes to `generations`. Admin-weighted past rounds get pulled back into the system prompt of future rounds matching the same motion + format. A nightly Haiku pass distills the top-rated rounds per format into a "PATTERNS THAT WORK" block that also feeds back into prompts. INTENDED behavior is that the AI on motion X today differs from the AI on motion X last month. As of 2026-08-11 that has never happened: `learning_distillations` is empty because only 3 rating labels exist in total and the distill needs 3 per format.

## 5. Voice & tone (how copy sounds)

**Punchy, specific, debater-register.** Not marketing-polished.

- Short sentences alongside longer ones. Rhythm matters.
- Real numbers and named people over abstraction. "Linda in Dayton loses her job" beats "economic harm to workers."
- Direct address. "You'll give real speeches against the clock" not "users can engage in speech-based interaction."
- No throat-clearing openers. Never start with "Imagine…" or "Picture a world…"
- No consultant-speak: no "holistic," "robust framework," "navigate the complexities of."
- Dismissive-when-earned. "That argument is cooked" is OK if the warrant is actually broken.
- One memorable line per section. Callbacks > recap closers.
- No em-dashes. Periods, commas, semicolons. that's the punctuation palette.

**Banned phrases (these rot the brand if they appear):**
- "no sign-up required" · "unlimited requests" (on Free) · "Infinity free"
- "Pay nothing" · "holistic approach" · "robust framework" · "at the end of the day"
- "Let's dive in" · "Let's unpack" · "Let's break it down" · "Let me break this down" · "Let me explain"
- "Hear me out" · "Stay with me" · "Bear with me"
- "In today's world" · "It's important to note"
- "ladies and gentlemen" · "I'm here to argue"
- "ChatGPT agrees with you" / "the chatbot agrees with you" / "yes-man chatbot" / any us-vs-ChatGPT zinger (the sneer reads as insecure — describe what we do, not what they don't)

(Note: "Free during beta" was on this list pre-2026-05-14 but has been lifted — the product is genuinely in beta with all tiers free, and the framing is explicitly "future pricing for reference, share feedback for pricing proposals." This is no longer a stale legacy reference; it's the current state.)

**The no-preface rule.** Never announce what you're about to say — say it. "Three reasons they're wrong, let's break it down" → "Three reasons they're wrong. One: ... Two: ... Three: ..." The numbers ARE the structure; the preface is dead weight. Same for "Here's why this fails" → cut "Here's why," start with the reason. Articulate, not chatty.

## 6. Build philosophy

- **Keep everything in this repo.** The landing, the app, the audience pages, the CSS, the Netlify functions. all in one tree. No split repos.
- **React inline in one big `app/index.html`.** Yes, it's 18k lines. Yes, we've talked about Vite. The split can happen when it actually hurts more than it helps; not before.
- **Netlify for everything**. hosting, functions, redirects. Stripe for payments. Firebase for auth + Firestore.
- **Publish dir is `/app/`** on Netlify's side. Mirror every user-facing file that lives at repo root into `/app/` too, because the build pulls from there. Same for `css/`.
- **`netlify.toml` in both root and `/app/`.** They must stay in sync on redirects. The `/` → `/landing.html` redirect needs `force = true` so it wins against any shadow `index.html`.
- **PWA + service worker.** Bump `CACHE_NAME` when HTML or bundle changes. Never precache `/` directly. it caused a root-routing regression.

## 10. Decision log (major decisions with why)

- **Entry is $20 with a waiver, and the tournament runs all day as drop-in. This SUPERSEDES the free-entry entries below** (2026-08-11, Aidan's call, confirmed twice in the same session). The entry model flipped four times in one day across parallel sessions: paid on 2026-08-10, then "Competing is free, permanently; advertising is the intended funding" (`522ee0a6`), then paid-with-a-waiver (`a75314ec`), then free again with the surfaces swept to match (`a8d5995d` and the log entry above it). Each flip was defensible on its own and each one left a surface behind, which is how the site ended up selling a $20 door that the endpoint refused. **The decision of record is now: $20 for prize eligibility, free entry plays the identical field, and the fee is waived for anyone who asks, on identical terms.** The waiver is what makes the fee defensible rather than exclusionary, so it is not a footnote: it renders on the paid door itself, not only in the rules. **Do not flip this again without an entry in this log that says why**, and if it does flip, the surfaces that must move together are: the two doors in `tournaments.html`, the eligibility and cost sections of `tournament-rules.html`, the landing `#tournament-band` facts, the poster PNGs (flat images, so their copy cannot follow the page), and `entryFeeCents` on the tournament doc.
  - **Promotion leads with the money; drop-in is a logistic, not a pitch** (2026-08-11, Aidan, same day). The entry model above is UNCHANGED. What changed is the landing band's headline, which read "Drop in any time. $850 in prizes." and now reads "Win $500 for winning an argument." Aidan's steer was blunt: do not advertise the drop-in aspect, advertise winning money. Convenience only sells a thing the reader has already decided to want, and a stranger scrolling past has not. The timing facts stay exactly where someone who HAS decided will look for them: the date line, the fact row, and `/tournaments`, whose "Turn up whenever. Debate. Leave." run-of-day section is deliberately untouched because it answers how the Saturday works rather than pitching it. `/tournaments`' own hero was already money-forward ("Get paid to win an argument") and needed nothing.
  - **Architecture note that outlives the price.** The 2026-08-10 failure was a door hidden by a JS branch reading `entryFeeCents`, so stale data resurrected a payment promise. The lesson taken from it then was "delete the paid door", and the durable version is narrower: **never let a data value decide whether a promise is on screen.** Both doors now render unconditionally in markup and the amount is stated in copy; the doc is set to match. If they ever disagree, checkout is the authority and the page is wrong, which is one line rather than an invisible branch.
  - **Format: all day, drop in whenever, no fixed start** (Aidan, same session). There is no check-in window and no round-one call. Doors open 10:00 AM ET, entrants arrive when it suits them, get paired, play as many rounds as they like, and standings come from the rounds they actually played. Entries close in the evening, the top of the board goes to single elimination, and the final streams. This is the /spar liquidity fix applied to an event: a fixed start time on a small base means everyone has to be free at the same hour, and an all-day pool only needs two people awake at once. **Open mechanical question, flagged not solved: `lib/tournament.mjs` pairs globally per ROUND over a ranked field, which assumes a synchronous round structure.** Drop-in play needs either continuous pairing (the spar matchmaker, with results counting toward the board) or rounds that are really time-windows. The copy now promises drop-in; the engine has not been reconciled with it, and that is the next real build, not a copy edit.

- **The AI judge stops being a single point of failure: published rubric, three-family panel, human appeal, fee neutrality, permanent audit** (2026-07-30). The judge was the one component where our own thesis conceded a hole: AI-judge resolution is circular. That was survivable while it was only an epistemic problem. It stops being survivable the moment credits, standing, or money settle off a verdict, because "an AI judge the operator owns, tunes, and can silently update" is house control of the outcome, which is the argument that sank Papaya and the argument a plaintiff's lawyer makes in the first paragraph. Five coupled changes, all state in Firestore.
  - **(1) The rubric is published before the round, versioned, and HASHED.** New `lib/judge-charter.mjs` is a pure module holding a season calendar and the plain-language criteria the adjudication core implements: the ten tests, the six-step weighing order, the four scorecard axes, the deadlock ladder, and the five things the judge may not do (no model repair, no invented arguments, no rewarding a flatly false unanswered claim, no deciding on what the judge would have argued, no debater instruction that names a winner). `rubricHash()` is a sha256 over a KEY-SORTED canonical serialization, so the digest follows content rather than object literal order: a reformat does not read as a rubric change and a real change cannot hide behind one. Every judgment now stamps `seasonId`, `rubricVersion`, and `rubricHash` **as of when it was judged**, not as of today, so a July ballot stays attributable to July's criteria forever. `RUBRIC_VERSION` is gone as a constant; that was the thing that made the rubric a moving target. The calendar is append-only and contiguous, with the pre-2026-07-30 era recorded honestly as `published:false` rather than retroactively reclassified under the new rubric.
  - **(2) The judge is a panel of three model families, not one call.** The season pins `claude-sonnet-5`, `gpt-4o`, and `gemini-2.0-flash` (the ids this repo's own brain proxies pin). Three calls to ONE model would measure sampling temperature and produce a flattering agreement number that means nothing, because the correlated error is the model itself; three families measure whether the round was decidable. Majority carries, medians (not means) set the points so one outlier juror cannot drag a score, and the load-bearing rule is that **an even split is NOT tie-broken**: any tie-break rule is a thumb on the scale and it would be our thumb, so a split records `resolution:'unresolved'`, no winner, the ladder does not move, and any market voids at face value. Refunding is the only side-neutral way to close a round nobody can decide. Panel calls run concurrently so wall clock is the slowest juror, not the sum, which keeps a three-family panel inside the sweep's existing budget. **Degradation is disclosed, never silent:** a missing provider key or a failed juror runs the panel short, and if quorum cannot be reached the ballot falls back to a single judge STAMPED `degraded`, surfaced by the public charter endpoint. `JUDGE_REQUIRE_PANEL=1` refuses instead of degrading; the default keeps rounds moving, because a missing key should not freeze every ballot on the site.
  - **(3) Inter-rater reliability is published, warts included.** `/api/judge/reliability` reports **Fleiss' kappa** on the binary winner rather than a raw agreement percentage, because raw agreement is inflated by any panel that leans one way: a judge that always said Prop would score 100% and measure nothing. Kappa nets that out. Published alongside it: the unanimity rate, the unresolved rate (a system that quietly resolved its split cases would show a better number and be worth less), the median juror spread in speaker points, and a count of any round where jurors did NOT receive identical prompts, which would make their agreement a measurement of the prompt. **Nothing is reported as a result under 30 rounds** (`reportable:false`); a kappa over four rounds is noise and dressing noise as documented reliability is the exact overclaim this system exists to avoid.
  - **(4) A human appeal layer, and no model may touch it.** Either debater can appeal within 72 hours on six stated grounds. Filing sets `disputeState:'open'`, which `verdictFrom` has always treated as a refusal and which nothing had ever set, so settlement genuinely freezes. A reviewer is disqualified by having debated the ROUND, not by role, so an admin who was in it cannot rule. Outcomes are upheld / overturned / void, a reason of 20+ characters is mandatory and public, and the original ballot is never deleted: it moves to `original` and the correction lives beside it, which is the only version that survives being subpoenaed. An overturn does not invent new speaker points, because a human reviewer flipping a call is not claiming to have re-scored four axes. **An appeal that cannot reach paid-out credits is decoration**, so this needed real reversal machinery: new `reverseMarket` in `lib/settle.mjs` writes negative compensating ledger entries namespaced by revision, un-settles positions so the corrected winner can be paid from the same intact pool, and backs out Sharp Score inputs; new `reverseRoundRating` in `lib/rating-apply.mjs` reverses the rating delta and the win/loss counts. Both state their limits on the record rather than faking a clean rewind: Glicko rd and volatility are path-dependent and are NOT reconstructed, and a streak is reset rather than guessed. Balances are allowed to go negative on a clawback, deliberately, because clamping at zero would make the ledger's arithmetic false and credits are non-redeemable so an underwater balance costs nothing real. `scripts/test-judge-integrity.mjs` asserts the appeal bench contains no provider call and no cron: routing an appeal back to a bigger model is the same circularity with a larger bill.
  - **(5) Fee neutrality made structural, and money gated on provenance.** Settlement is pari-mutuel with no rake term anywhere, so the operator earns the same whichever side wins. That is now enforced rather than promised: the test reads `credits.mjs` and `settle.mjs` and fails the build on any rake, fee, commission, vig, or house account. Verified adversarially by injecting a rake and confirming the guard fires. Coupled fix: **`MONEY_VERDICT_SOURCES` restricts settlement to `verdictSource:'server'`.** `judgment.mjs` has recorded provenance honestly since it was written and nothing acted on it; live-round ballots are written by a PARTICIPANT'S OWN BROWSER, and a verdict authored by one of the two interested parties settling an economy is worse than the house writing it. Stated consequence: **live human rounds do not settle credits until their ballot moves server-side.** The ladder and the public record are unaffected. No live impact today, since nothing creates `markets` documents yet, which made this the right moment to close it.
  - **Everything is logged, in the cloud, immutably.** New `judge_audit/{judgmentId}`: season, rubric version and hash, every juror's provider and model id, whether each model was the season pin or a **disclosed override**, every raw vote including the dissents, the panel outcome, a hash of the exact prompt each juror received, timings, and failures. Public read, server write, never updated in place; corrections append to a `revisions` subcollection, the same posture as the credit ledger. Full prompt text is deliberately NOT stored: it is ~60KB of identical adjudication core per juror per round, and the rubric hash plus the prompt hash plus the transcript already reproduce the input, so a copy on every row would be a large cost for no evidentiary gain.
  - **Surfaces:** public `/judge-integrity` renders the live charter and reliability figures from the two open endpoints (so the page cannot drift from what the judge is actually pinned to), linked from `/judge` and `/judge-paradigms` and in the sitemap. `/api/judge/charter` and `/api/judge/reliability` are open and keyless so anyone can fetch the charter, hash it, and confirm the criteria that judged a round. Round pages now publish panel agreement next to the verdict and render dissenting jurors with their reasoning intact rather than blended into the majority RFD. **Bug caught while wiring this:** `rounds.html` read the winner as `winner==='prop' ? prop : opp`, so a null winner would have announced the Opposition as victor, which is the precise opposite of what a split panel means; guarded, along with the sweep's ballot email.
  - **Guarded by a hook, not just a test.** `scripts/test-judge-integrity.mjs` (145 assertions) joins the price guard in `scripts/hooks/pre-commit` and hard-blocks a commit that breaks any of these promises, because each one is published to users and quotable in a dispute. Two of its guards were found VACUOUS by deliberately weakening the code they cover: the 1-1 split passes on quorum rather than on the majority rule, so the real 2-2 case (clears quorum, still must not resolve) was untested and is now covered. Worth repeating as a habit: a test suite that passes on the first run has not yet been shown to test anything.

- **The default format is now a spec'd lay format, judged out of 30** (2026-07-28). Quick Clash was the format nobody had written down: 3/3/2/2 with 2 minutes of prep, four rules of one sentence each, and a ballot scored on the APDA 23-30 speaker scale, which is a circuit instrument being pointed at people who have never seen a round. Aidan's one-page lay spec replaces it. **Structure:** constructive then rebuttal, one each a side. The sheet says 5/5/5/5 with 5 minutes prep; shipped as **5/5 constructives, 3/3 rebuttals, 5 minutes prep**, taking the sheet's own flagged risk ("maybe a shorter rebuttal?") rather than a 25-minute default. The four `rules` strings were rewritten to carry the shape: the second constructive does two jobs (answer their case, then build its own), rebuttals bring no new arguments, and every argument is claim then warrant then impact. **Scoring:** a 30-point lay ballot, 15 for the overall argument, 10 for responses to the opponent's case, 5 for structure and comprehension. Highest total wins, so **a weaker case can take the round on responses plus structure**, and the judge is told to open the RFD by saying so when that is what happened. `speakerPoints` stays on the 23-30 scale and stays out of the decision, because the leaderboard series is comparable across formats and a 22/30 rubric total posted into it would read as an incoherent round. The rubric rides a `rubric` key on the ballot JSON and renders as a scorecard under the verdict (and in the text export); if a brain omits it, the ballot renders exactly as before. **The plumbing this needed already existed and was dead:** `singleJudgeBallot` has carried a `{{formatJudgingCriteria}}` placeholder since it moved server-side, and no caller ever filled it, so the literal token was being sent to the model on every ballot. All three call sites in `practice.html` now pass `fmt.judging`, which makes per-format judging criteria a one-key-per-format edit for any future format. **Same rubric on the human surfaces:** live-round's `quick` (the "General" default) got the speech times and the 30-point JUDGE_RULES, and /judge's `quick` got the rubric in prose. **Cost note, deliberate:** AI speech length scales off `speech.time` at ~170 wpm, so the default format's AI turns grew roughly 65% in tokens. - **2v2 partner matching, a real tournament engine, and spectator admission** (2026-07-28). Three coupled builds that turn "we could host a tournament" into infrastructure. Closes the open item left by the 2026-07-27 tournament-readiness pass, which ended: *a real bracket/schedule data model once an actual tournament is scheduled (none exists yet)*. **(1) 2v2 partner matching.** A 2v2 needs a partner, an opposing team, and four people awake at once. `/partners` + `partner-match.mjs` own the first: a duo forms either by invite code or through an open `partner_pool` with a mutual-accept handshake, and it NEVER auto-completes the way a spar match does, because a partner is a bigger commitment than an opponent. `duo-pair.mjs` owns the second, and the load-bearing decision there is that the queue doc is keyed by **team, not user**: per-user docs would make a 2v2 a four-way atomic write where any half-commit strands a real person in a room their partner never entered, while team-keyed it is the same two-document transaction spar-pair already runs in production. Either partner can queue the team and the other does not need to be at their screen, because requiring four simultaneous humans instead of two plus two means it never fires at this traffic level. Every cross-user write is admin-SDK server-side, the lesson spar-pair was rebuilt on. **(2) live-round seats two per bench.** A 2v2 stays a two-BENCH round (format spec, transcript tags and ballot all reason about gov vs opp), so partner uids ride the existing benches rather than a new four-side model. Verified per format: APDA correctly gives seat 1 the PM and the PMR. **BP/Worlds in a four-person room is a COMPRESSION, not real 4-team BP, and is labelled as such.** **(3) tournament engine.** `lib/tournament.mjs` is pure and deterministic: seeded, so a draw can be reproduced when a coach asks why they hit the same opponent twice. It pairs **globally over the ranked field, not per win-bracket** — per-bracket pairing looks right and fails late, because two teams share a record precisely because they just debated, so small brackets fill with prior opponents and the only move left is the rematch (measured: 4 rematches in a 12-team field over 5 rounds, side skew reaching 3). Side pressure sits inside the matching plus a 2-opt repair, since choosing the partner is what keeps sides even; assigning sides to a fixed pairing can only pick the lesser of two evils. 1045 assertions in `scripts/test-tournament-pairing.mjs`. Odd fields are allowed side skew 2 on purpose: the bye breaks side parity and flattening it would cost an unearned pull-up, which is the worse trade. **(4) spectator admission.** Watching was open; anyone with a link dropped into the audience and debaters learned from the count ticking up. - **Tournament load: voice metered per user, spectator counting made linear** (2026-07-28). A readiness audit ahead of running real tournaments found two things that break at venue scale, both invisible at today's traffic. **(1) The voice rate limit locked out whole buildings.** `realtime-session.mjs` ran `checkRateLimit('rt_' + ip)` at 6/hour and 10/day BEFORE the auth and plan gate, with no exemption for signed-in or paying callers. Anonymous accounts are free and unlimited to mint, so that replaced a hard 6/hour/IP ceiling with NO ceiling: rotate a throwaway anonymous account and the IP layers never apply again. Per-account damage was bounded at 2 by `FREE_VOICE_LIFETIME_LIMIT`, but the accounts themselves were not bounded, and `/api/realtime-session` mints real billable OpenAI Realtime sessions (confirmed by curling it unauthenticated: App Check is not enforcing because the site key was never filled in, so the IP layers are the ONLY gate on that endpoint). The token is verified once and reused by the usage gate, not twice. **The limiter also only pretended to work:** it was a module-scope `Map`, so each Netlify isolate kept its own counter and a cold start wiped it. Under light traffic that reads as a working limit; under load it is a lottery. The per-UID keying fix works regardless. Same pass fixed a spoofable key: the IP now prefers Netlify's `x-nf-client-connection-ip` over the client-supplied `x-forwarded-for`, whose whole raw string was previously the rate-limit key, so one caller could mint unlimited distinct keys by varying a header. **(2) Spectator counting was quadratic.** Every viewer on `/live-round` ran an unbounded `onSnapshot` over the `watchers` subcollection while every spectator wrote a heartbeat every 30s, so the reads behind the "N watching" pill grew as N SQUARED: roughly 5K reads/min at 50 spectators, 80K at 200, 500K at 500 (about $18/hour for a counter), and every browser in the room reprocessed all N documents on every beat, which is what actually made big rooms janky. The obvious fix is Firestore's `count()` aggregation, and it does not exist here: **aggregation is modular-SDK only, and `Query.count` is undefined on the compat builds this page loads. Verified in-browser against 10.5.0 and 11.10.0; do not "restore" it without re-checking that.** Instead the count folds into the presence beat that seated debaters already write to the round doc every 30s, and every client in the room already listens to that doc, so distributing the number is free. Cost is now about 4N reads/min total regardless of audience size (two debaters reading N watcher docs per beat) and a spectator pays nothing: about 2K reads/min at 500 spectators against the old 500K. The freshness filter runs server-side on `ts`, so stale heartbeats never cross the wire; `ts` is a plain field so the automatic single-field index covers it, verified with a live query (no composite index to deploy). Capped at 1000 with a "1000+" display so one enormous room cannot quietly become the expensive case again. Deliberate trade-off: with nobody seated (spectator-only replay viewing) the number holds its last value instead of dropping to zero, because a frozen count beats a wrong one. **(3) A ballot had exactly one chance to succeed.** `generateBallot()` in `live-round.html` was a single fetch with no retry, and its failure card offered a "Try again" button wired to `location.reload()`. A tournament ends many rounds inside the same minute, so that endpoint takes a burst of 8000-token Sonnet calls at the moment it matters most; `claude.mjs` passes the Anthropic response straight through, so one upstream 429 or 529 ended the round with no ballot and a button that reloaded the page back into the same overloaded window. Now bounded to 4 attempts with backoff at roughly 2s, 5s, and 12s, each JITTERED, because N clients retrying on an identical schedule is just a second thundering herd. An explicit `Retry-After` is honored (capped at 30s). Retryable: 408, 425, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 529, plus network failures and PARSE failures, since a truncated or malformed generation is exactly the transient case a retry fixes and the attempt budget bounds the token cost. Auth and plan errors (401/402/403/400) fail fast because they never self-heal. Worst case is about 20s of retrying before the failure card appears, and that card now retries in place instead of reloading, keeps the round view, and says plainly that the transcript is saved. New `live_round_ballot_failed` GA4 event carries status and attempt count so a real tournament leaves evidence. **Not a code fix but load-bearing operationally: anonymous callers share `claude.mjs`'s per-IP anon caps (5/min, 15/hour, 30/day) across a whole venue, and signed-in users bypass them entirely. Everyone signs in before an event, or the building splits 30 requests a day.** Audited alongside and found clean: the deployed Firestore ruleset was byte-identical to `origin/main` (checked against the Rules API, not assumed), coach emails are correctly segregated into admin-only `atlas_contacts`, `public_profiles` carries no contact fields by design, and Daily rooms cap at 8 participants but spectators never join the video, so that is a debater-plus-judge ceiling and not an audience limit.

- **Audience cameras: verified spectators can be seen, never heard** (2026-08-10). Debaters could see a watcher COUNT and nothing else, so a live round felt like speaking into a void even with people in it. Spectators can now opt into appearing on camera in the same Daily room, and the whole design is built around the fact that inviting strangers' cameras into a room with minors in it is the part that can go badly. **The mic is not a setting, it is an impossibility.** `/api/audience-cam` mints a Daily meeting token with `permissions.canSend:['video']`; the refusal to publish audio happens at Daily's media server, so a hostile client running patched JavaScript still cannot open a mic. A UI toggle would have been a promise; this is a property. **Five gates before a token is issued:** (1) NAMED account only, because Firebase anonymous accounts are free and unlimited to mint (see the 2026-07-28 rate-limit entry) and an anonymous camera is an unaccountable camera, so `sign_in_provider != 'anonymous'` is required; (2) Cloudflare Turnstile, which **fails CLOSED**: with `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` / `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` unset the entire feature is unavailable and the client never renders the camera button, because shipping a stranger-camera feature with its human check quietly skipped is the one degradation that is not acceptable here (a configured-but-unreachable Cloudflare is a different case and fails OPEN, since the other four gates hold); (3) `video_bans`, using the SAME salted-uid-and-IP derivation as create-daily-room, so a strike earned as a debater blocks the camera here too; (4) a rate limit of 12/hour and 40/day per uid through the shared `lib/rate-limit.mjs`; (5) a cap of 4 concurrent cameras per round, counted server-side from fresh `camAt` heartbeats on the watchers subcollection (the same 75s liveness window the watch count uses, so it needs no new index and no new collection). On top of the token, the camera runs the SAME on-device `nsfw-guard.js` watchdog debater cameras run, with the identical two-consecutive-samples trip and scores-only reporting. **The debaters hold a kill switch.** `allowAudienceCams` on the round doc, flipped from a third option in the existing visibility menu, defaults on; false makes the server refuse new tokens AND drops live audience cameras client-side through the round snapshot every client already holds. Participant-only by the existing `live_rounds` update rule, so no rules change was needed; `camAt` rides the watcher's own doc, which the existing watchers rule already scopes to `watcherUid == request.auth.uid`. **CPU was a first-class constraint, not an afterthought,** because four extra faces land on the same machines already running a Daily call, an avatar canvas at 24fps, and a TF.js classifier every 1.6s. Audience cameras publish at 320x240/12fps (texture, not broadcast), and every audience tile is pinned to the lowest simulcast layer via `updateReceiveSettings` on BOTH the debater and audience frames, matched by the `Audience · ` user_name prefix the token stamps. Daily's prebuilt layout promotes the active speaker and an audience token can never send audio, so a downgraded audience tile can never be the thing anyone is trying to look at. **Bug found while sizing this, and it was live:** `max_participants` was 8 with a comment calling it a flood guard for debaters, but every SPECTATOR joins the same Daily room receive-only, so the ceiling was really two debaters plus six watchers and the seventh person to open any live round got a full room. Raised to 24; flooding is handled where it belongs (unlisted rounds, the admission gate, and this function's own cap of 4). Because create-daily-room is create-or-fetch, existing rooms keep the old ceiling until they expire, within 24h. Same pass: `/floor`'s server poll and `/arena`'s three demo-stage tickers now skip hidden tabs, matching what every other timer on those pages already did. **Ops: audience cameras are OFF on arrival and stay off until four env vars exist** (`DAILY_API_KEY`, `DAILY_DOMAIN`, `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY`, `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`). Setting the two Turnstile keys is what switches the feature on, with no redeploy. **Not built:** audience-camera moderation from the debaters' side beyond the kill switch (no per-face eject yet, the round-wide switch is the blunt instrument for now).

- **Anonymous analytics keyed by device, not by browser tab; visitor counter un-padded and un-broken** (2026-08-10). Nothing in the signed-out path had a stable identity, which made the anon half of every dashboard a count of TABS. `log-event.mjs` minted its synthetic uid as `anon:` + the per-tab sessionStorage id, so a returning visitor, a second tab, and a reload after a crash were three different "anonymous users" — and anon retention was therefore unmeasurable by construction, for the population that dominates traffic while retention is the stated #1 problem (§8). The durable id already existed (`localStorage._da_aid`, minted by track.js and sent as `metadata.anon_id` for the funnel-join work); the server simply never keyed on it. Now it does, falling back to the session id when localStorage is blocked (Safari ITP, in-app browsers) so those visitors degrade to the old per-tab behavior instead of vanishing, with `deviceKeyed` on the row so a reader can tell a real device from a fallback. **DISCONTINUITY, deliberate: unique anon counts step DOWN from this date.** That is the correction, not a regression; do not compare a window that spans it. /admin relabels the funnel from "uids" to "subjects" and defines a subject as one account OR one anon device, with the date noted in the help panel. **Two things about `/api/visitor-tick` found while verifying, both older than this change.** (1) Its cumulative `count` was padded with a hardcoded `BASELINE = 7074` from the ad-spike era, floored so the number could never read below it. That is a claims-discipline problem on a public surface, so the pad is now subtracted back out on read; the stored doc is left intact as the record of what it used to claim. Un-padded, the true all-time figure is **7**. (2) The whole function had been DEAD for months: per-day counters were addressed as `metrics/daily/{YYYY-MM-DD}`, three segments, which Firestore reads as a collection path and rejects as a document, so `db.doc()` threw on construction and every read and write went to the catch. No daily doc was ever written, `count30d` was always 0, and `public-join-history`'s `totalVisits` was structurally pinned at 0 — which is why /community always rendered its static `data-baseline` fallback. `public-join-history.mjs` already carried a TODO diagnosing this precisely ("has been silently throwing for months") and proposing the flatten; taking that fix, `metrics_daily/{YYYY-MM-DD}`. Nothing to migrate, since the old path never held data. Caught by probing the deployed endpoint rather than by reading it: it answered `source: baseline-fallback`, which only happens in the catch. **A tick now also requires a `deviceId` to increment**, because the "already counted" marker lives in the same localStorage a blocking browser refuses, so those visitors were re-ticking on every page load; they are now undercounted instead, which is the honest direction to be wrong in for a number we might quote. **Standing lesson: a metric that renders a plausible number is not evidence that it works.** Both of these shipped a believable figure to a public page for months — one padded, one frozen — and neither surfaced as an error anywhere.

- **A round room does not open until both debaters prove they are there** (2026-08-11). Measured across all 411 live rounds ever created: only ~85 had both people actually present, 215 had exactly one, 111 had neither. 390 never completed a single speech, and median room lifetime was 1.5 minutes. Debater names are written at MATCH time, not on arrival, so a round document looked complete while one chair had been empty the whole time, and every downstream number inherited the lie. **The round itself was never the problem: past the first speech, ~43% of rounds finish (9 of 21).** We were matching people against nobody, and the person who DID show up sat alone and left. That is the retention story, and it is not a top-of-funnel problem. **Two causes, both fixed.** (1) The consent gate from 2026-06-12 only fired when someone filed a judge-paradigm note or the two sides proposed different formats; every other pair flipped straight to `matched` with no evidence anyone was at a keyboard. It now fires for EVERY foreground pair, and both `consents` start false. The old rule auto-yessed the side with nothing to review, which is exactly the side that could be an empty chair: a debater who filed no note was consented into a room without ever proving presence. (2) **This supersedes the 2026-06-15 "B" auto-accept tuning.** That change made the deciding card auto-JOIN at zero when the tab was visible, so a present-but-slow user wouldn't lose a rare match. Its own comment named the risk exactly right ("accepting there would strand the opponent alone") and then trusted `visibilityState` to rule it out. It does not: a foregrounded tab nobody is sitting at is `visible`, so for 15 seconds an empty chair could accept a match. Timeouts now always auto-PASS, which routes into the ghost path already built in the 2026-06-12 hardening (server cancels the silent side, requeues the live one on a clean doc). Trading a few extra matches for rooms that open onto nobody is a bad trade at this liquidity, because the person we lose is the one who showed up. A pure presence check gets its own card ("X is ready" / "I'm here, start the round") rather than the review card, which is worded for weighing something; notes and cross-format pairs keep their review cards, and background "Spar live" pairs still skip the gate because `js/notifications.js` has no card to render. **Deliberately NOT built, because it already existed:** the in-room dead-opponent rescue. `live-round.html` has carried a 28s grace, a "Looking for your debater" strip, and a no-show prompt (Message them / Find another / Leave / Keep waiting) for a while. It worked fine. It was simply being handed rooms that never had two people in them. **Lesson: a record written at intent time is not evidence of the thing happening.** Both debater names, the room id, the motion and the format were all populated and correct on 411 rounds; not one of those fields meant anyone had arrived.

- **Free entry finished: the surfaces caught up with the decision, and the metering bug behind it** (2026-08-11). Paid tournament entry was retired on 2026-08-10 in `entry-checkout.mjs`, but only the endpoint learned about it, so for a day the site sold a $20 door that answered `410 ENTRY_IS_FREE`. **What was still live:** `/tournaments` rendered "Paid entry / $20 / Pay in with card" because the paid door was hidden only by a JS branch that ran when `entryFeeCents` was 0, and the doc still carried 2000; the same page's FAQ and JSON-LD already said "There is no entry fee and no card" beside it; and `/tournament-rules`, the published rules of an $850 cash contest, still said free entry was "Not eligible for cash prizes" and that an entry is prize-eligible "if it paid in or was granted a fee waiver". That last one is the one that mattered: it told a free entrant who wins first place that the title is theirs and the $500 goes to someone else, which is the opposite of the decision, on the document a dispute would be argued from. **The fix that generalizes: the paid door was DELETED from the markup, not hidden, and no copy on either page reads `entryFeeCents` at all.** Hiding on a data value is precisely what let stale data resurrect a payment promise; with one entry model there is nothing for stale data to flip. Rules rewritten so eligibility turns on placement and age and nothing else, section 6 became "Cost" (no fees, so nothing to refund), and the liability clause no longer caps at an entry fee nobody pays. Verified by serving the pages the REAL live doc with its fee still at 2000: no pay button, no `$20`, one door. Then the doc itself was corrected (`entryFeeCents` 2000 → 0, description rewritten off the "$20 pay-in makes your entry prize-eligible" line that `/tournament` renders as its lede); `paidEntries` was 0 at the time, so no money was stranded. **Same session, the bug that started it:** `/practice` metered one free credit per `callAI` rather than per ROUND, and a Quick Clash round makes ~19 calls, so at `ANON_LIMIT = 1` a guest could never finish their first round. The second call threw the quota error, which rendered mid-debate as "AI couldn't respond. Guest preview used. Sign in..." and then auto-advanced through empty speeches into a ballot judging nothing. Now a round charges once at `beginRoundCredit()` in `goToSpeeches` and everything inside it runs free through the ballot; out of credit shows the CTA before the round starts instead of breaking it halfway. **Do not "fix" future quota complaints by raising the caps** (1 anon / 10 signed-in) — those numbers are correct as rounds and were set deliberately on 2026-05-18. Two riders found the same way: anonymous Firebase accounts were being read as signed-in on `/practice` (`js/notifications.js` mints one for the background Spar pill on every page), which gave guests the signed-in cap, suppressed every sign-in nudge, saved rounds under a uid they can never sign back into, and sent anonymous tokens to the account endpoints; and the prep-timer auto-advance effect had an empty body, so prep counted to 0:00 and sat there looking frozen. **Standing lesson from both halves: a surface that renders a plausible screen is not evidence the rule behind it is right.** The $20 button looked like a working checkout, and the round looked like it was running, right up to the point where each contradicted a decision that had already been made.
