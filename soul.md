## 1. What this is (one sentence)

Debatable is a **live arena for spoken argument, open to anyone who wants to argue something out.** Competitive debate is where the engine is sharpest and professionals are a second room, but neither is the door (see §2, rewritten 2026-08-12). Lives at **itsdebatable.com** (canonical since 2026-07-22; debateai.com and the other legacy domains 301 there).

**Brand system:** the product name is **"Debatable"**, hosted at **itsdebatable.com**. It is the only public name across visible copy, metadata, structured data, generated audio, native labels, and extensions. CTAs describe the action instead of acting as a second brand. The canonical full-round surface is `/practice`; retired public URLs only survive as server-side redirects.

Not a chatbot. Not a writing assistant. Not a research tool. A **live sparring partner** that gives you real speeches against a clock, then grades you like a judge would.

## 3. The wedge (what makes it different)

Five things, in order of importance:

1. **Format accuracy.** Policy gets tagged-card delivery. LD gets value/criterion. BP gets extensions + whip structure. APDA is impromptu with no fake citations. No other AI-debate tool does this. they all speak in generic "Harvard debate society" English. This is the hardest moat to copy because it requires actually knowing debate.

2. **Voice round + judge RFD.** You can run a timed speech, hear pushback, take POIs, and get a judge ballot. That's the moat against ChatGPT. voice + timer + format-specific structure.

3. **Built from inside the activity.** The product was written by someone who competed, and it shows in the format rules, the voice banks, and the judge paradigms. **The founder is ANONYMOUS on every public surface as of 2026-08-22** (see the decision log): no name, no school, no title, no year, no photo. Sell the depth, never the credential. "Built from inside competitive debate" is the sanctioned phrasing; "a national APDA champion at UChicago" is not, anywhere.

4. **Six brains + HD voice on paid.** Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Open Lab. DeepSeek (added 2026-05-15) routes direct to the DeepSeek API and pins to `deepseek-chat`; Open Lab (added same day) routes through OpenRouter and defaults to Nous Hermes 4 405B with an env-overrideable pool that also covers Mistral Large, Qwen3-235B, and Llama 4 Maverick/Scout. The $10/year Individual tier is where users get the full engine. BYOK is Anthropic-only and labeled as such. (Currently in beta — every tier is $0 while we figure out pricing; see §7.)

5. **A learning loop wired to sharpen from rated rounds** (capture live since 2026-05-13; the nightly distillation has NEVER produced an artifact, see the 2026-08-11 decision-log entry, so do not claim it externally). Every typed AI turn and every voice-round transcript writes to `generations`. Admin-weighted past rounds get pulled back into the system prompt of future rounds matching the same motion + format. A nightly Haiku pass distills the top-rated rounds per format into a "PATTERNS THAT WORK" block that also feeds back into prompts. INTENDED behavior is that the AI on motion X today differs from the AI on motion X last month. As of 2026-08-11 that has never happened: `learning_distillations` is empty because only 3 rating labels exist in total and the distill needs 3 per format.

## 5. Voice & tone (how copy sounds)

**Punchy, specific, debater-register.** Not marketing-polished.

- Short sentences alongside longer ones. Rhythm matters.
- Real numbers and named people over abstraction. "Linda in Dayton loses her job" beats "economic harm to workers."
- Direct address. "You'll give real speeches against the clock" not "users can engage in speech-based interaction."
- No throat-clearing openers. Never start with "Imagine…" or "Picture a world…"
- No consultant-speak: no "holistic," "robust framework," "navigate the complexities of."
- Dismissive-when-earned. "That argument is cooked" is OK if the warrant is actually broken.
- One memorable line per section. Callbacks > recap closers.
- No em-dashes. Periods, commas, semicolons. that's the punctuation palette.

**Banned phrases (these rot the brand if they appear):**
- "no sign-up required" · "unlimited requests" (on Free) · "Infinity free"
- "Pay nothing" · "holistic approach" · "robust framework" · "at the end of the day"
- "Let's dive in" · "Let's unpack" · "Let's break it down" · "Let me break this down" · "Let me explain"
- "Hear me out" · "Stay with me" · "Bear with me"
- "In today's world" · "It's important to note"
- "ladies and gentlemen" · "I'm here to argue"
- "ChatGPT agrees with you" / "the chatbot agrees with you" / "yes-man chatbot" / any us-vs-ChatGPT zinger (the sneer reads as insecure — describe what we do, not what they don't)

(Note: "Free during beta" was on this list pre-2026-05-14 but has been lifted — the product is genuinely in beta with all tiers free, and the framing is explicitly "future pricing for reference, share feedback for pricing proposals." This is no longer a stale legacy reference; it's the current state.)

**The no-preface rule.** Never announce what you're about to say — say it. "Three reasons they're wrong, let's break it down" → "Three reasons they're wrong. One: ... Two: ... Three: ..." The numbers ARE the structure; the preface is dead weight. Same for "Here's why this fails" → cut "Here's why," start with the reason. Articulate, not chatty.

## 6. Build philosophy

- **Keep everything in this repo.** The landing, the app, the audience pages, the CSS, the Netlify functions. all in one tree. No split repos.
- **React inline in one big `app/index.html`.** Yes, it's 18k lines. Yes, we've talked about Vite. The split can happen when it actually hurts more than it helps; not before.
- **Netlify for everything**. hosting, functions, redirects. Stripe for payments. Firebase for auth + Firestore.
- **Publish dir is `/app/`** on Netlify's side. Mirror every user-facing file that lives at repo root into `/app/` too, because the build pulls from there. Same for `css/`.
- **`netlify.toml` in both root and `/app/`.** They must stay in sync on redirects. The `/` → `/landing.html` redirect needs `force = true` so it wins against any shadow `index.html`.
- **PWA + service worker.** Bump `CACHE_NAME` when HTML or bundle changes. Never precache `/` directly. it caused a root-routing regression.

- **Cash rounds: real money, paid at the door, and the fee is the whole legal argument** (2026-08-22, Aidan: "we need to seriously rethink the betting section to be more simple, we want real money, so you pay us first"). The betting surface sold three lanes and two markets, one of which (the Ladder) had no inventory and could not be traded, and **not one of the five could take a card**. There are now five economies in this repo (floor_*, predict_*, credits/settle, value_*, and this) and this is the only one that moves real money on the result of an argument, so read this entry before touching any of them.

  **The shape, and why it is this shape.** Two people buy in for the same amount, argue one motion, and the winner takes the pot. That is a CONTEST OF SKILL: the people paying are the people competing, and the outcome turns on which of them argues better. The thing that was NOT built, deliberately and permanently, is the obvious-looking alternative: selling points so spectators can wager cash on rounds. That is unlicensed sports betting in every US state, and it is worse here than generically, because we run the judge that settles it. A house that owns the oracle and takes a cut of the bet is the argument that sank Papaya and the first paragraph of any complaint. **Do not build it, and do not let a future "simplification" collapse the two.**

  **THE FEE IS CHARGED AT THE DOOR, and that is what makes it compatible with the no-rake invariant rather than an exception to it.** The judge charter forbids a rake in `credits.mjs` and `settle.mjs` for a stated reason: "the operator's take cannot depend on who wins." A flat 20% taken from both sides before a word is spoken does not depend on who wins. It is the same number whichever way the ballot goes, so the house is indifferent to the result by construction rather than by promise. `feeOf` takes the buy-in and nothing else, and the guard asserts the arity so nobody can widen it. **What stays forbidden is a cut at SETTLEMENT**: `awardPot` takes the pot and the winner, does no fee arithmetic, and throws if the payout is a cent short. A skim there is a stake in a verdict our own judge wrote.

  **A round that produces no verdict refunds everything, the fee included.** Nobody paid to sit in a queue. `refundPlan` returns gross and the Stripe refund passes no amount, so the whole charge goes back; we only earn on a round that actually gets judged. A split panel voids rather than being tie-broken, because a tie-break would be the house's thumb on a contest the house is holding the money for.

  **Money never moves on an AI ballot alone.** A verdict marks the winner owed; paying is gated a second time on `APPEAL_WINDOW_MS`, the same constant the appeal layer publishes, so the money and the right to object cannot disagree. The winner is read from the recorded judgment, never from the request body, and only on `verdictSource:'server'`. Payouts are sent by hand, same as tournament prizes and bounty pots, because there is still no Stripe Connect and no KYC. **That is the real ceiling on this feature: it works at ten rounds a week and not at a hundred a day.** **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-22 by the payout-rail entry at the top of this log: Connect and KYC now exist, so the send is a Stripe transfer rather than a manual errand. - **The AI judge stops being a single point of failure: published rubric, three-family panel, human appeal, fee neutrality, permanent audit** (2026-07-30). The judge was the one component where our own thesis conceded a hole: AI-judge resolution is circular. That was survivable while it was only an epistemic problem. It stops being survivable the moment credits, standing, or money settle off a verdict, because "an AI judge the operator owns, tunes, and can silently update" is house control of the outcome, which is the argument that sank Papaya and the argument a plaintiff's lawyer makes in the first paragraph. Five coupled changes, all state in Firestore.
  - **(1) The rubric is published before the round, versioned, and HASHED.** New `lib/judge-charter.mjs` is a pure module holding a season calendar and the plain-language criteria the adjudication core implements: the ten tests, the six-step weighing order, the four scorecard axes, the deadlock ladder, and the five things the judge may not do (no model repair, no invented arguments, no rewarding a flatly false unanswered claim, no deciding on what the judge would have argued, no debater instruction that names a winner). `rubricHash()` is a sha256 over a KEY-SORTED canonical serialization, so the digest follows content rather than object literal order: a reformat does not read as a rubric change and a real change cannot hide behind one. Every judgment now stamps `seasonId`, `rubricVersion`, and `rubricHash` **as of when it was judged**, not as of today, so a July ballot stays attributable to July's criteria forever. `RUBRIC_VERSION` is gone as a constant; that was the thing that made the rubric a moving target. The calendar is append-only and contiguous, with the pre-2026-07-30 era recorded honestly as `published:false` rather than retroactively reclassified under the new rubric.
  - **(2) The judge is a panel of three model families, not one call.** The season pins `claude-sonnet-5`, `gpt-4o`, and `gemini-2.0-flash` (the ids this repo's own brain proxies pin). Three calls to ONE model would measure sampling temperature and produce a flattering agreement number that means nothing, because the correlated error is the model itself; three families measure whether the round was decidable. Majority carries, medians (not means) set the points so one outlier juror cannot drag a score, and the load-bearing rule is that **an even split is NOT tie-broken**: any tie-break rule is a thumb on the scale and it would be our thumb, so a split records `resolution:'unresolved'`, no winner, the ladder does not move, and any market voids at face value. Refunding is the only side-neutral way to close a round nobody can decide. Panel calls run concurrently so wall clock is the slowest juror, not the sum, which keeps a three-family panel inside the sweep's existing budget. **Degradation is disclosed, never silent:** a missing provider key or a failed juror runs the panel short, and if quorum cannot be reached the ballot falls back to a single judge STAMPED `degraded`, surfaced by the public charter endpoint. `JUDGE_REQUIRE_PANEL=1` refuses instead of degrading; the default keeps rounds moving, because a missing key should not freeze every ballot on the site.
  - **(3) Inter-rater reliability is published, warts included.** `/api/judge/reliability` reports **Fleiss' kappa** on the binary winner rather than a raw agreement percentage, because raw agreement is inflated by any panel that leans one way: a judge that always said Prop would score 100% and measure nothing. Kappa nets that out. Published alongside it: the unanimity rate, the unresolved rate (a system that quietly resolved its split cases would show a better number and be worth less), the median juror spread in speaker points, and a count of any round where jurors did NOT receive identical prompts, which would make their agreement a measurement of the prompt. **Nothing is reported as a result under 30 rounds** (`reportable:false`); a kappa over four rounds is noise and dressing noise as documented reliability is the exact overclaim this system exists to avoid.
  - **(4) A human appeal layer, and no model may touch it.** Either debater can appeal within 72 hours on six stated grounds. Filing sets `disputeState:'open'`, which `verdictFrom` has always treated as a refusal and which nothing had ever set, so settlement genuinely freezes. A reviewer is disqualified by having debated the ROUND, not by role, so an admin who was in it cannot rule. Outcomes are upheld / overturned / void, a reason of 20+ characters is mandatory and public, and the original ballot is never deleted: it moves to `original` and the correction lives beside it, which is the only version that survives being subpoenaed. An overturn does not invent new speaker points, because a human reviewer flipping a call is not claiming to have re-scored four axes. **An appeal that cannot reach paid-out credits is decoration**, so this needed real reversal machinery: new `reverseMarket` in `lib/settle.mjs` writes negative compensating ledger entries namespaced by revision, un-settles positions so the corrected winner can be paid from the same intact pool, and backs out Sharp Score inputs; new `reverseRoundRating` in `lib/rating-apply.mjs` reverses the rating delta and the win/loss counts. Both state their limits on the record rather than faking a clean rewind: Glicko rd and volatility are path-dependent and are NOT reconstructed, and a streak is reset rather than guessed. Balances are allowed to go negative on a clawback, deliberately, because clamping at zero would make the ledger's arithmetic false and credits are non-redeemable so an underwater balance costs nothing real. `scripts/test-judge-integrity.mjs` asserts the appeal bench contains no provider call and no cron: routing an appeal back to a bigger model is the same circularity with a larger bill.
  - **(5) Fee neutrality made structural, and money gated on provenance.** Settlement is pari-mutuel with no rake term anywhere, so the operator earns the same whichever side wins. That is now enforced rather than promised: the test reads `credits.mjs` and `settle.mjs` and fails the build on any rake, fee, commission, vig, or house account. Verified adversarially by injecting a rake and confirming the guard fires. Coupled fix: **`MONEY_VERDICT_SOURCES` restricts settlement to `verdictSource:'server'`.** `judgment.mjs` has recorded provenance honestly since it was written and nothing acted on it; live-round ballots are written by a PARTICIPANT'S OWN BROWSER, and a verdict authored by one of the two interested parties settling an economy is worse than the house writing it. Stated consequence: **live human rounds do not settle credits until their ballot moves server-side.** The ladder and the public record are unaffected. - **The default format is now a spec'd lay format, judged out of 30** (2026-07-28). Quick Clash was the format nobody had written down: 3/3/2/2 with 2 minutes of prep, four rules of one sentence each, and a ballot scored on the APDA 23-30 speaker scale, which is a circuit instrument being pointed at people who have never seen a round. Aidan's one-page lay spec replaces it. **Structure:** constructive then rebuttal, one each a side. The sheet says 5/5/5/5 with 5 minutes prep; shipped as **5/5 constructives, 3/3 rebuttals, 5 minutes prep**, taking the sheet's own flagged risk ("maybe a shorter rebuttal?") rather than a 25-minute default. The four `rules` strings were rewritten to carry the shape: the second constructive does two jobs (answer their case, then build its own), rebuttals bring no new arguments, and every argument is claim then warrant then impact. **Scoring:** a 30-point lay ballot, 15 for the overall argument, 10 for responses to the opponent's case, 5 for structure and comprehension. Highest total wins, so **a weaker case can take the round on responses plus structure**, and the judge is told to open the RFD by saying so when that is what happened. `speakerPoints` stays on the 23-30 scale and stays out of the decision, because the leaderboard series is comparable across formats and a 22/30 rubric total posted into it would read as an incoherent round. - **Tournament load: voice metered per user, spectator counting made linear** (2026-07-28). A readiness audit ahead of running real tournaments found two things that break at venue scale, both invisible at today's traffic. **(1) The voice rate limit locked out whole buildings.** `realtime-session.mjs` ran `checkRateLimit('rt_' + ip)` at 6/hour and 10/day BEFORE the auth and plan gate, with no exemption for signed-in or paying callers. Retryable: 408, 425, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 529, plus network failures and PARSE failures, since a truncated or malformed generation is exactly the transient case a retry fixes and the attempt budget bounds the token cost. Auth and plan errors (401/402/403/400) fail fast because they never self-heal.
