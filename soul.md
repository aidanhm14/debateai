## 1. What this is (one sentence)

Debatable is a **live arena for spoken argument, open to anyone who wants to argue something out.** Competitive debate is where the engine is sharpest and professionals are a second room, but neither is the door (see §2, rewritten 2026-08-12). Lives at **itsdebatable.com** (canonical since 2026-07-22; debateai.com and the other legacy domains 301 there).

**Brand system:** the product name is **"Debatable"**, hosted at **itsdebatable.com**. It is the only public name across visible copy, metadata, structured data, generated audio, native labels, and extensions. CTAs describe the action instead of acting as a second brand. The canonical full-round surface is `/practice`; retired public URLs only survive as server-side redirects.

Not a chatbot. Not a writing assistant. Not a research tool. A **live sparring partner** that gives you real speeches against a clock, then grades you like a judge would.

## 2. Who it's for

**ANYONE WHO WANTS TO ARGUE SOMETHING OUT (Aidan's call, 2026-08-12).**
This reverses the old "primary audience = competitive debaters" framing
below, which is kept underneath as the sharpest USE CASE, not as the
door. His words: the platform is not for professional debaters, it is
for anyone. Copy rules that follow from it, and they are enforceable:

- **Do not call the crowd "debaters" in audience-facing copy.** Say
  people. The landing was swept on 2026-08-12 (live globe caption,
  section nav, face-wall label, footer, community notes, FAQ section
  head, leaderboard column, JSON-LD descriptions, plus `topbar.js`
  "Find debaters and clubs" which rides ~85 pages). "Debater" survives
  only where it names a ROLE inside a round, and in the founder's own
  credential, which is a bio fact rather than a gate.
- **Never gate the reader on knowing a format.** A stranger should not
  have to decode "Asian Parli" before they understand what a round is.
  Formats are what the product is good at; they are not the invitation.
- **The three things the pitch leads with now:** anyone can argue,
  you can become influential, and you can debate a streamer because
  the matchups pay high XP.

**On the streamer promise, claims discipline applies.** The path is
real and shipped (leaderboard standing feeds the challenger pool for a
creator matchup, `/tournaments` plus the creator-sweepstakes band). No
creator round has actually happened yet. So write it as the thing you
can earn, never as a thing that has occurred, and never name a creator
as a participant. The creator-watchlist portraits are explicitly

one of them is CC-licensed or public domain with attribution in
`app/img/creator-watchlist/README.md`. Do not put an unlicensed photo
of a real person on any surface, and do not caption a real person with

## 3. The wedge (what makes it different)

Five things, in order of importance:

1. **Format accuracy.** Policy gets tagged-card delivery. LD gets value/criterion. BP gets extensions + whip structure. APDA is impromptu with no fake citations. No other AI-debate tool does this. they all speak in generic "Harvard debate society" English. This is the hardest moat to copy because it requires actually knowing debate.

2. **Voice round + judge RFD.** You can run a timed speech, hear pushback, take POIs, and get a judge ballot. That's the moat against ChatGPT. voice + timer + format-specific structure.

3. **Built by someone who won.** The creator is a national APDA champion. Most AI-product founders can say "I used AI to build this"; very few can say "I actually argued in this format at the top of the country." Surface this on the landing, not just the footer.

4. **Six brains + HD voice on paid.** Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Open Lab. DeepSeek (added 2026-05-15) routes direct to the DeepSeek API and pins to `deepseek-chat`; Open Lab (added same day) routes through OpenRouter and defaults to Nous Hermes 4 405B with an env-overrideable pool that also covers Mistral Large, Qwen3-235B, and Llama 4 Maverick/Scout. The $10/year Individual tier is where users get the full engine. BYOK is Anthropic-only and labeled as such. (Currently in beta — every tier is $0 while we figure out pricing; see §7.)

5. **A learning loop wired to sharpen from rated rounds** (capture live since 2026-05-13; the nightly distillation has NEVER produced an artifact, see the 2026-08-11 decision-log entry, so do not claim it externally). Every typed AI turn and every voice-round transcript writes to `generations`. Admin-weighted past rounds get pulled back into the system prompt of future rounds matching the same motion + format. A nightly Haiku pass distills the top-rated rounds per format into a "PATTERNS THAT WORK" block that also feeds back into prompts. INTENDED behavior is that the AI on motion X today differs from the AI on motion X last month. As of 2026-08-11 that has never happened: `learning_distillations` is empty because only 3 rating labels exist in total and the distill needs 3 per format.

## 4. Non-negotiables (product principles)

These are the rules that override other considerations. Any change that violates one needs an explicit rationale.

- **Auth is ADVISED, not required (reversed 2026-04-20).** Anyone can use the app anonymously with the 5-request cap; sign-in unlocks the persistent profile, the 250-request Individual tier, and the style-learning loop. The prior full-screen pricing gate blocked too many users whose browser couldn't load Firebase (Safari ITP, in-app browsers, strict extensions). Nudges live in the topbar "Sign in. free" button, the SignUpNudgeModal after 2+ generations, and feature-specific paywalls on limit-hit. Google, an emailed sign-in link, and email/password are live on web. Native also offers Apple. **Lowering the barrier to entry beats path-count tidiness** (2026-08-12): the old "two paths = confusion" rule was written when the choice was Google or nothing, and it is not a reason to make someone invent a password. **ONE EXCEPTION, added 2026-08-18: live human pairing requires a named account** (/spar, /debate-chat, and the background "Spar live" pill). The other side of a live round is a real person, so an unaccountable opponent is a cost paid by someone other than the visitor. The AI surfaces are unchanged and still let a stranger in without an account. See the decision-log entry for the three doors this closed.
- **5-request free tier, then paywall.** Anonymous: 5. Signed-in: 10 total (5 anon + 5 bonus). The 5→15 bump on 2026-05-14 was a conversion-funnel move that didn't survive a credit-burn audit four days later. - **BYOK is Claude-only.** Label it Claude-only on the card, the input form, and the error message. Cross-provider attempts throw a specific error.
- **APDA does NOT belong in the Topics Hub.** It's impromptu, no rolling motion. Only PF / LD / Policy / Congress belong there. APDA users go to the Motions tab (random generator).

Machine-readable identity says so everywhere (JSON-LD applicationCategory: SocialNetworkingApplication, manifest categories social/entertainment) and new copy leads with the social loop, never with practice/training/learning outcomes. AI stays the infrastructure (judge, ballot, fallback opponent), never the headline. The ed-framed SEO funnel pages (/learn, /practice, /schools, Counter) survive as acquisition doors that capture ed search intent, but they funnel INTO the arena and don't define the product.

- **No villain-line zingers about ChatGPT / "chatbots" / "yes-man AI."** Positive product framing only — describe what Debatable does, not what other tools allegedly fail at. The "us vs them" sneer reads as insecure and was swept out 2026-05-14. "How is this different from a general-purpose AI" answers the same question without naming a villain.
- **Hard-refresh after SW bumps.** When cached bundles need to expire, bump `CACHE_NAME` in both `sw.js` files. Current: `debateos-v12`.
- **Debate-first is the commitment (2026-04-20).** The six separate audience pages (lawyers / sales / teams / founders / students / speakers) were consolidated into a single `/pro` page. ## 5. Voice & tone (how copy sounds)

**Punchy, specific, debater-register.** Not marketing-polished.

- Short sentences alongside longer ones. Rhythm matters.
- Real numbers and named people over abstraction. "Linda in Dayton loses her job" beats "economic harm to workers."
- Direct address. "You'll give real speeches against the clock" not "users can engage in speech-based interaction."
- No throat-clearing openers. Never start with "Imagine…" or "Picture a world…"
- No consultant-speak: no "holistic," "robust framework," "navigate the complexities of."
- Dismissive-when-earned. "That argument is cooked" is OK if the warrant is actually broken.
- One memorable line per section. Callbacks > recap closers.
- No em-dashes. Periods, commas, semicolons. that's the punctuation palette.

**Banned phrases (these rot the brand if they appear):**
- "no sign-up required" · "unlimited requests" (on Free) · "Infinity free"
- "Pay nothing" · "holistic approach" · "robust framework" · "at the end of the day"
- "Let's dive in" · "Let's unpack" · "Let's break it down" · "Let me break this down" · "Let me explain"
- "Hear me out" · "Stay with me" · "Bear with me"
- "In today's world" · "It's important to note"
- "ladies and gentlemen" · "I'm here to argue"
- "ChatGPT agrees with you" / "the chatbot agrees with you" / "yes-man chatbot" / any us-vs-ChatGPT zinger (the sneer reads as insecure — describe what we do, not what they don't)

(Note: "Free during beta" was on this list pre-2026-05-14 but has been lifted — the product is genuinely in beta with all tiers free, and the framing is explicitly "future pricing for reference, share feedback for pricing proposals." This is no longer a stale legacy reference; it's the current state.)

**The no-preface rule.** Never announce what you're about to say — say it. "Three reasons they're wrong, let's break it down" → "Three reasons they're wrong. One: ... Two: ... Three: ..." The numbers ARE the structure; the preface is dead weight. Same for "Here's why this fails" → cut "Here's why," start with the reason. Articulate, not chatty.

## 6. Build philosophy

- **Keep everything in this repo.** The landing, the app, the audience pages, the CSS, the Netlify functions. all in one tree. No split repos.
- **React inline in one big `app/index.html`.** Yes, it's 18k lines. Yes, we've talked about Vite. The split can happen when it actually hurts more than it helps; not before.
- **Netlify for everything**. hosting, functions, redirects. Stripe for payments. Firebase for auth + Firestore.
- **Publish dir is `/app/`** on Netlify's side. Mirror every user-facing file that lives at repo root into `/app/` too, because the build pulls from there. Same for `css/`.
- **`netlify.toml` in both root and `/app/`.** They must stay in sync on redirects. The `/` → `/landing.html` redirect needs `force = true` so it wins against any shadow `index.html`.
- **PWA + service worker.** Bump `CACHE_NAME` when HTML or bundle changes. Never precache `/` directly. it caused a root-routing regression.

Guest queueing is retired on every live-pairing surface. The gate on /spar now routes everyone into the shared chooser (Google, emailed link, email/password) instead of offering "Start as guest". **The button was the smallest part of it.** `js/notifications.js` signs nearly every visitor in anonymously for the bell, so `currentUser` was almost always truthy, and three separate places read that as signed in: (1) /spar's boot handler dropped the anonymous session straight into `joinQueue()`, (2) the background "Spar live" availability pill accepted any user (`isQueueUser` was `!!u`) and `ensureQueueUser` MINTED an anonymous account on demand when there wasn't one, so the queue could be joined from any page on the site, and (3) `/debate-chat` pairs strangers into a casual room off the same `matchmaking_queue` collection and hid its own sign-in gate on the same truthy check. So a "signed-out" visitor was already queueing, already being matched with real people, and never had a reason to sign up. All three now gate on `isNamedUser()`, and `joinQueue()` on both pages is the single choke point every entry path funnels through (find button, autoqueue from casual-room's "Next opponent", the 400ms requeue, the deep links). **This is the same trap as the 2026-07-28 rate-limit correction and the memory note behind it: an anonymous Firebase user is not a signed-in user, and `!!currentUser` on this site means "has a browser", not "has an account".** Verified on production with a live anonymous session: the gate renders, no queue doc is written, the pill is hidden, and clicking it opens the chooser rather than enlisting the guest. **CLOSED SAME DAY on Aidan's go-ahead ("trust you fixing the problems"), and the audit that followed found the gate was one of five doors, not three.** A read-only sweep of every other pairing surface turned up: (4) **`/live` MINTED an anonymous account to seat someone in a live debate** ("Creating your guest identity" on clicking an empty debater seat), which is precisely the empty-chair failure the 2026-08-11 ready-check work exists to prevent, since nobody can ever sign back into that account; the same page let a guest take the judge seat, post a challenge to the public board, and accept someone else's. The advise-not-require principle still governs the AI surfaces (/practice, voice), where a stranger can still try the product without an account. Live human pairing is the exception, and the reason it is defensible is that the other person in the round is a real person who deserves an accountable counterpart. - **The AI judge stops being a single point of failure: published rubric, three-family panel, human appeal, fee neutrality, permanent audit** (2026-07-30). The judge was the one component where our own thesis conceded a hole: AI-judge resolution is circular. That was survivable while it was only an epistemic problem. It stops being survivable the moment credits, standing, or money settle off a verdict, because "an AI judge the operator owns, tunes, and can silently update" is house control of the outcome, which is the argument that sank Papaya and the argument a plaintiff's lawyer makes in the first paragraph. Five coupled changes, all state in Firestore.
  - **(1) The rubric is published before the round, versioned, and HASHED.** New `lib/judge-charter.mjs` is a pure module holding a season calendar and the plain-language criteria the adjudication core implements: the ten tests, the six-step weighing order, the four scorecard axes, the deadlock ladder, and the five things the judge may not do (no model repair, no invented arguments, no rewarding a flatly false unanswered claim, no deciding on what the judge would have argued, no debater instruction that names a winner). `rubricHash()` is a sha256 over a KEY-SORTED canonical serialization, so the digest follows content rather than object literal order: a reformat does not read as a rubric change and a real change cannot hide behind one. Every judgment now stamps `seasonId`, `rubricVersion`, and `rubricHash` **as of when it was judged**, not as of today, so a July ballot stays attributable to July's criteria forever. `RUBRIC_VERSION` is gone as a constant; that was the thing that made the rubric a moving target. The calendar is append-only and contiguous, with the pre-2026-07-30 era recorded honestly as `published:false` rather than retroactively reclassified under the new rubric.
  - **(2) The judge is a panel of three model families, not one call.** The season pins `claude-sonnet-5`, `gpt-4o`, and `gemini-2.0-flash` (the ids this repo's own brain proxies pin). Three calls to ONE model would measure sampling temperature and produce a flattering agreement number that means nothing, because the correlated error is the model itself; three families measure whether the round was decidable. Majority carries, medians (not means) set the points so one outlier juror cannot drag a score, and the load-bearing rule is that **an even split is NOT tie-broken**: any tie-break rule is a thumb on the scale and it would be our thumb, so a split records `resolution:'unresolved'`, no winner, the ladder does not move, and any market voids at face value. Refunding is the only side-neutral way to close a round nobody can decide. Kappa nets that out. Published alongside it: the unanimity rate, the unresolved rate (a system that quietly resolved its split cases would show a better number and be worth less), the median juror spread in speaker points, and a count of any round where jurors did NOT receive identical prompts, which would make their agreement a measurement of the prompt. **Nothing is reported as a result under 30 rounds** (`reportable:false`); a kappa over four rounds is noise and dressing noise as documented reliability is the exact overclaim this system exists to avoid.

Outcomes are upheld / overturned / void, a reason of 20+ characters is mandatory and public, and the original ballot is never deleted: it moves to `original` and the correction lives beside it, which is the only version that survives being subpoenaed. An overturn does not invent new speaker points, because a human reviewer flipping a call is not claiming to have re-scored four axes. **An appeal that cannot reach paid-out credits is decoration**, so this needed real reversal machinery: new `reverseMarket` in `lib/settle.mjs` writes negative compensating ledger entries namespaced by revision, un-settles positions so the corrected winner can be paid from the same intact pool, and backs out Sharp Score inputs; new `reverseRoundRating` in `lib/rating-apply.mjs` reverses the rating delta and the win/loss counts. - **Tournament load: voice metered per user, spectator counting made linear** (2026-07-28). A readiness audit ahead of running real tournaments found two things that break at venue scale, both invisible at today's traffic. **(1) The voice rate limit locked out whole buildings.** `realtime-session.mjs` ran `checkRateLimit('rt_' + ip)` at 6/hour and 10/day BEFORE the auth and plan gate, with no exemption for signed-in or paying callers. Anonymous accounts are free and unlimited to mint, so that replaced a hard 6/hour/IP ceiling with NO ceiling: rotate a throwaway anonymous account and the IP layers never apply again. Per-account damage was bounded at 2 by `FREE_VOICE_LIFETIME_LIMIT`, but the accounts themselves were not bounded, and `/api/realtime-session` mints real billable OpenAI Realtime sessions (confirmed by curling it unauthenticated: App Check is not enforcing because the site key was never filled in, so the IP layers are the ONLY gate on that endpoint). The token is verified once and reused by the usage gate, not twice. **The limiter also only pretended to work:** it was a module-scope `Map`, so each Netlify isolate kept its own counter and a cold start wiped it. Under light traffic that reads as a working limit; under load it is a lottery. Same pass fixed a spoofable key: the IP now prefers Netlify's `x-nf-client-connection-ip` over the client-supplied `x-forwarded-for`, whose whole raw string was previously the rate-limit key, so one caller could mint unlimited distinct keys by varying a header. **(2) Spectator counting was quadratic.** Every viewer on `/live-round` ran an unbounded `onSnapshot` over the `watchers` subcollection while every spectator wrote a heartbeat every 30s, so the reads behind the "N watching" pill grew as N SQUARED: roughly 5K reads/min at 50 spectators, 80K at 200, 500K at 500 (about $18/hour for a counter), and every browser in the room reprocessed all N documents on every beat, which is what actually made big rooms janky. The obvious fix is Firestore's `count()` aggregation, and it does not exist here: **aggregation is modular-SDK only, and `Query.count` is undefined on the compat builds this page loads. Verified in-browser against 10.5.0 and 11.10.0; do not "restore" it without re-checking that.** Instead the count folds into the presence beat that seated debaters already write to the round doc every 30s, and every client in the room already listens to that doc, so distributing the number is free. Cost is now about 4N reads/min total regardless of audience size (two debaters reading N watcher docs per beat) and a spectator pays nothing: about 2K reads/min at 500 spectators against the old 500K. The freshness filter runs server-side on `ts`, so stale heartbeats never cross the wire; `ts` is a plain field so the automatic single-field index covers it, verified with a live query (no composite index to deploy). Capped at 1000 with a "1000+" display so one enormous room cannot quietly become the expensive case again. Deliberate trade-off: with nobody seated (spectator-only replay viewing) the number holds its last value instead of dropping to zero, because a frozen count beats a wrong one. **(3) A ballot had exactly one chance to succeed.** `generateBallot()` in `live-round.html` was a single fetch with no retry, and its failure card offered a "Try again" button wired to `location.reload()`. Now bounded to 4 attempts with backoff at roughly 2s, 5s, and 12s, each JITTERED, because N clients retrying on an identical schedule is just a second thundering herd. An explicit `Retry-After` is honored (capped at 30s). Retryable: 408, 425, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 529, plus network failures and PARSE failures, since a truncated or malformed generation is exactly the transient case a retry fixes and the attempt budget bounds the token cost. Auth and plan errors (401/402/403/400) fail fast because they never self-heal. Worst case is about 20s of retrying before the failure card appears, and that card now retries in place instead of reloading, keeps the round view, and says plainly that the transcript is saved. New `live_round_ballot_failed` GA4 event carries status and attempt count so a real tournament leaves evidence. **Not a code fix but load-bearing operationally: anonymous callers share `claude.mjs`'s per-IP anon caps (5/min, 15/hour, 30/day) across a whole venue, and signed-in users bypass them entirely. The standalone listen-or-paste judge previously hardcoded Claude Sonnet and mentioned paradigms only in a non-functional explainer below the FAQ. It now separates three choices: (1) judge style, the audience lens (match format / lay / flow / technical / communication); (2) decision paradigm (automatic / tabula rasa / policymaker / stock issues / games player / truth seeker / hypothesis tester / communication / custom); and (3) AI brain (Claude / GPT / Gemini / Grok / DeepSeek / Open Lab). - **Audience cameras: verified spectators can be seen, never heard** (2026-08-10). Debaters could see a watcher COUNT and nothing else, so a live round felt like speaking into a void even with people in it. Spectators can now opt into appearing on camera in the same Daily room, and the whole design is built around the fact that inviting strangers' cameras into a room with minors in it is the part that can go badly. **The mic is not a setting, it is an impossibility.** `/api/audience-cam` mints a Daily meeting token with `permissions.canSend:['video']`; the refusal to publish audio happens at Daily's media server, so a hostile client running patched JavaScript still cannot open a mic. A UI toggle would have been a promise; this is a property. **Five gates before a token is issued:** (1) NAMED account only, because Firebase anonymous accounts are free and unlimited to mint (see the 2026-07-28 rate-limit entry) and an anonymous camera is an unaccountable camera, so `sign_in_provider != 'anonymous'` is required; (2) Cloudflare Turnstile, which **fails CLOSED**: with `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` / `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` unset the entire feature is unavailable and the client never renders the camera button, because shipping a stranger-camera feature with its human check quietly skipped is the one degradation that is not acceptable here (a configured-but-unreachable Cloudflare is a different case and fails OPEN, since the other four gates hold); (3) `video_bans`, using the SAME salted-uid-and-IP derivation as create-daily-room, so a strike earned as a debater blocks the camera here too; (4) a rate limit of 12/hour and 40/day per uid through the shared `lib/rate-limit.mjs`; (5) a cap of 4 concurrent cameras per round, counted server-side from fresh `camAt` heartbeats on the watchers subcollection (the same 75s liveness window the watch count uses, so it needs no new index and no new collection). On top of the token, the camera runs the SAME on-device `nsfw-guard.js` watchdog debater cameras run, with the identical two-consecutive-samples trip and scores-only reporting. **The debaters hold a kill switch.** `allowAudienceCams` on the round doc, flipped from a third option in the existing visibility menu, defaults on; false makes the server refuse new tokens AND drops live audience cameras client-side through the round snapshot every client already holds. Participant-only by the existing `live_rounds` update rule, so no rules change was needed; `camAt` rides the watcher's own doc, which the existing watchers rule already scopes to `watcherUid == request.auth.uid`. **CPU was a first-class constraint, not an afterthought,** because four extra faces land on the same machines already running a Daily call, an avatar canvas at 24fps, and a TF.js classifier every 1.6s. Audience cameras publish at 320x240/12fps (texture, not broadcast), and every audience tile is pinned to the lowest simulcast layer via `updateReceiveSettings` on BOTH the debater and audience frames, matched by the `Audience · ` user_name prefix the token stamps. Daily's prebuilt layout promotes the active speaker and an audience token can never send audio, so a downgraded audience tile can never be the thing anyone is trying to look at. **Bug found while sizing this, and it was live:** `max_participants` was 8 with a comment calling it a flood guard for debaters, but every SPECTATOR joins the same Daily room receive-only, so the ceiling was really two debaters plus six watchers and the seventh person to open any live round got a full room. Raised to 24; flooding is handled where it belongs (unlisted rounds, the admission gate, and this function's own cap of 4). Because create-daily-room is create-or-fetch, existing rooms keep the old ceiling until they expire, within 24h. Same pass: `/floor`'s server poll and `/arena`'s three demo-stage tickers now skip hidden tabs, matching what every other timer on those pages already did. **Ops: audience cameras are OFF on arrival and stay off until four env vars exist** (`DAILY_API_KEY`, `DAILY_DOMAIN`, `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY`, `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`). Setting the two Turnstile keys is what switches the feature on, with no redeploy. **Not built:** audience-camera moderation from the debaters' side beyond the kill switch (no per-face eject yet, the round-wide switch is the blunt instrument for now).

- **Anonymous analytics keyed by device, not by browser tab; visitor counter un-padded and un-broken** (2026-08-10). Nothing in the signed-out path had a stable identity, which made the anon half of every dashboard a count of TABS. `log-event.mjs` minted its synthetic uid as `anon:` + the per-tab sessionStorage id, so a returning visitor, a second tab, and a reload after a crash were three different "anonymous users" — and anon retention was therefore unmeasurable by construction, for the population that dominates traffic while retention is the stated #1 problem (§8). The durable id already existed (`localStorage._da_aid`, minted by track.js and sent as `metadata.anon_id` for the funnel-join work); the server simply never keyed on it. Now it does, falling back to the session id when localStorage is blocked (Safari ITP, in-app browsers) so those visitors degrade to the old per-tab behavior instead of vanishing, with `deviceKeyed` on the row so a reader can tell a real device from a fallback. **DISCONTINUITY, deliberate: unique anon counts step DOWN from this date.** That is the correction, not a regression; do not compare a window that spans it. /admin relabels the funnel from "uids" to "subjects" and defines a subject as one account OR one anon device, with the date noted in the help panel. **Two things about `/api/visitor-tick` found while verifying, both older than this change.** (1) Its cumulative `count` was padded with a hardcoded `BASELINE = 7074` from the ad-spike era, floored so the number could never read below it. That is a claims-discipline problem on a public surface, so the pad is now subtracted back out on read; the stored doc is left intact as the record of what it used to claim. Un-padded, the true all-time figure is **7**. (2) The whole function had been DEAD for months: per-day counters were addressed as `metrics/daily/{YYYY-MM-DD}`, three segments, which Firestore reads as a collection path and rejects as a document, so `db.doc()` threw on construction and every read and write went to the catch. No daily doc was ever written, `count30d` was always 0, and `public-join-history`'s `totalVisits` was structurally pinned at 0 — which is why /community always rendered its static `data-baseline` fallback. `public-join-history.mjs` already carried a TODO diagnosing this precisely ("has been silently throwing for months") and proposing the flatten; taking that fix, `metrics_daily/{YYYY-MM-DD}`. Nothing to migrate, since the old path never held data. Caught by probing the deployed endpoint rather than by reading it: it answered `source: baseline-fallback`, which only happens in the catch. **A tick now also requires a `deviceId` to increment**, because the "already counted" marker lives in the same localStorage a blocking browser refuses, so those visitors were re-ticking on every page load; they are now undercounted instead, which is the honest direction to be wrong in for a number we might quote. **Standing lesson: a metric that renders a plausible number is not evidence that it works.** Both of these shipped a believable figure to a public page for months — one padded, one frozen — and neither surfaced as an error anywhere.

- **A round room does not open until both debaters prove they are there** (2026-08-11). Measured across all 411 live rounds ever created: only ~85 had both people actually present, 215 had exactly one, 111 had neither. 390 never completed a single speech, and median room lifetime was 1.5 minutes. Debater names are written at MATCH time, not on arrival, so a round document looked complete while one chair had been empty the whole time, and every downstream number inherited the lie. **The round itself was never the problem: past the first speech, ~43% of rounds finish (9 of 21).** We were matching people against nobody, and the person who DID show up sat alone and left. That is the retention story, and it is not a top-of-funnel problem. **Two causes, both fixed.** (1) The consent gate from 2026-06-12 only fired when someone filed a judge-paradigm note or the two sides proposed different formats; every other pair flipped straight to `matched` with no evidence anyone was at a keyboard. It now fires for EVERY foreground pair, and both `consents` start false. The old rule auto-yessed the side with nothing to review, which is exactly the side that could be an empty chair: a debater who filed no note was consented into a room without ever proving presence. (2) **This supersedes the 2026-06-15 "B" auto-accept tuning.** That change made the deciding card auto-JOIN at zero when the tab was visible, so a present-but-slow user wouldn't lose a rare match. Its own comment named the risk exactly right ("accepting there would strand the opponent alone") and then trusted `visibilityState` to rule it out. It does not: a foregrounded tab nobody is sitting at is `visible`, so for 15 seconds an empty chair could accept a match. Timeouts now always auto-PASS, which routes into the ghost path already built in the 2026-06-12 hardening (server cancels the silent side, requeues the live one on a clean doc). Trading a few extra matches for rooms that open onto nobody is a bad trade at this liquidity, because the person we lose is the one who showed up. A pure presence check gets its own card ("X is ready" / "I'm here, start the round") rather than the review card, which is worded for weighing something; notes and cross-format pairs keep their review cards, and background "Spar live" pairs still skip the gate because `js/notifications.js` has no card to render. **Deliberately NOT built, because it already existed:** the in-room dead-opponent rescue. `live-round.html` has carried a 28s grace, a "Looking for your debater" strip, and a no-show prompt (Message them / Find another / Leave / Keep waiting) for a while. It worked fine. It was simply being handed rooms that never had two people in them. **Lesson: a record written at intent time is not evidence of the thing happening.** Both debater names, the room id, the motion and the format were all populated and correct on 411 rounds; not one of those fields meant anyone had arrived.

- **The nightly learning loop has never once run, and the claim came off the pitch** (2026-08-11). `learning_distillations` holds zero documents, and not because the cron is broken. `scheduled-distill.mjs` requires `MIN_EXAMPLES = 3` per format drawn from generations rated ≥4 or saved. Live counts: 153 generations captured (85 in the last 30 days, still writing today), **0 rated ≥4, 2 saved, 1 carrying any rating at all.** With three labels ever created across eighteen formats, every format returns `skipped` every night, permanently. `NIGHTLY_PAUSED` is not set; the job runs and finds nothing. The newest rating signal is 2026-05-23, one day BEFORE the /voice-rfd rating widget shipped, so that widget has produced zero labels in its entire life. So §3 wedge #5's "the AI on motion X today is meaningfully different from the AI on motion X last month" has never been true. Capture works; the learn-back half has never produced an artifact. `report.html` (investor-facing) and `landing-classic.html` no longer assert it: they now say the loop is built to sharpen from rated rounds and state plainly that no learned-pattern block has shipped into a prompt to date. **Do not restore the flat claim until `learning_distillations` actually holds documents.** Sequencing note, and it is the reason not to chase this directly: ratings are downstream of FINISHED rounds, and rounds were not finishing (see the ready-check entry above). Fix arrival, then completion, and labels become possible; going hunting for labels first would be treating a symptom.

- **Free entry finished: the surfaces caught up with the decision, and the metering bug behind it** (2026-08-11). Paid tournament entry was retired on 2026-08-10 in `entry-checkout.mjs`, but only the endpoint learned about it, so for a day the site sold a $20 door that answered `410 ENTRY_IS_FREE`. **What was still live:** `/tournaments` rendered "Paid entry / $20 / Pay in with card" because the paid door was hidden only by a JS branch that ran when `entryFeeCents` was 0, and the doc still carried 2000; the same page's FAQ and JSON-LD already said "There is no entry fee and no card" beside it; and `/tournament-rules`, the published rules of an $850 cash contest, still said free entry was "Not eligible for cash prizes" and that an entry is prize-eligible "if it paid in or was granted a fee waiver". That last one is the one that mattered: it told a free entrant who wins first place that the title is theirs and the $500 goes to someone else, which is the opposite of the decision, on the document a dispute would be argued from. **The fix that generalizes: the paid door was DELETED from the markup, not hidden, and no copy on either page reads `entryFeeCents` at all.** Hiding on a data value is precisely what let stale data resurrect a payment promise; with one entry model there is nothing for stale data to flip. Rules rewritten so eligibility turns on placement and age and nothing else, section 6 became "Cost" (no fees, so nothing to refund), and the liability clause no longer caps at an entry fee nobody pays. Verified by serving the pages the REAL live doc with its fee still at 2000: no pay button, no `$20`, one door. Then the doc itself was corrected (`entryFeeCents` 2000 → 0, description rewritten off the "$20 pay-in makes your entry prize-eligible" line that `/tournament` renders as its lede); `paidEntries` was 0 at the time, so no money was stranded. **Same session, the bug that started it:** `/practice` metered one free credit per `callAI` rather than per ROUND, and a Quick Clash round makes ~19 calls, so at `ANON_LIMIT = 1` a guest could never finish their first round. The second call threw the quota error, which rendered mid-debate as "AI couldn't respond. Guest preview used. Sign in..." and then auto-advanced through empty speeches into a ballot judging nothing. Now a round charges once at `beginRoundCredit()` in `goToSpeeches` and everything inside it runs free through the ballot; out of credit shows the CTA before the round starts instead of breaking it halfway. **Do not "fix" future quota complaints by raising the caps** (1 anon / 10 signed-in) — those numbers are correct as rounds and were set deliberately on 2026-05-18. Two riders found the same way: anonymous Firebase accounts were being read as signed-in on `/practice` (`js/notifications.js` mints one for the background Spar pill on every page), which gave guests the signed-in cap, suppressed every sign-in nudge, saved rounds under a uid they can never sign back into, and sent anonymous tokens to the account endpoints; and the prep-timer auto-advance effect had an empty body, so prep counted to 0:00 and sat there looking frozen. **Standing lesson from both halves: a surface that renders a plausible screen is not evidence the rule behind it is right.** The $20 button looked like a working checkout, and the round looked like it was running, right up to the point where each contradicted a decision that had already been made.

- **Spectator search: /watch was 140 crawlable words, and a replay had no URL of its own** (2026-08-12). Aidan asked to rank for "watch people debate". The audit found the technical base already good (thoughtful robots.txt, dynamic sitemaps with honest lastmod, ~6,000 static words on the homepage) and the failure concentrated in exactly the surface built for that query. **Two problems, one per layer.**
  - **/watch promised video and delivered a spinner.** 140 static words wrapped around a grid filled by JS from a corpus that is EMPTY: 0 recordings, 0 clips, 1 published round sitewide. A crawler that skipped the fetch saw a page advertising replays and rendering "Loading replays…", which is the shape Google's helpful-content system demotes, and a human who searched for a debate to watch hit a dead end. Now ~750 words of plain HTML (three ways to watch, how to get into a live round, what a round actually is, 7 questions with FAQPage markup verbatim against the visible copy) that carries the page whether or not the corpus has anything in it, and an empty state that routes to /live and /spar and says WHY it is empty (recording is opt in).
  - **The competitive read is the reason this was worth doing, and it inverted the obvious plan.** theChatStage ranks for the same query with roughly 400 words of plain copy and our exact model (matched opponent, anonymous spectators). They are not winning on a video library, so this is winnable on content rather than on inventory. Debatable was already ranking for spectator long-tail, but via the HOMEPAGE, because /watch was too thin to be the answer.
  - **A recording had no address.** Sharing one handed out `/watch?r={id}`, a query string on a page whose canonical is bare `/watch`, so the equity from every share piled onto the hub and not one replay existed as a crawlable URL. New server-rendered `/w/{id}` per published recording, own canonical, VideoObject. **The thing that made the markup possible: `/w/{id}/play`,** a stable path that mints a fresh Daily link per request and 302s to it. The signed URL behind it expires within the hour and could never be published as `contentUrl`; the address in front of it never moves. Playback needs no JavaScript as a result, since `<video src>` follows the 302 itself, Range requests included. `sitemap-recordings.xml` joins the index and falls back to /watch while empty, since a zero-entry urlset reads as a broken sitemap in Search Console. Replay cards became anchors (crawlable path, cmd-click opens the standalone page, plain click still plays inline) and Copy link hands out `/w/{id}`.
  - **What was deliberately NOT emitted, and it is the same discipline as the /reviews entry above.** No VideoObject or ItemList on /watch itself: there is no video there, and marking up media a visitor cannot find is what earns a manual action rather than a rich result. It belongs on a per-recording page, which is why one now exists.
  - **The thumbnail gap, and why YouTube closes it.** `/w/{id}` shipped valid VideoObject that was NOT rich-result eligible, because Google wants a thumbnail representing the video and the fallback is the brand OG card; nothing in the pipeline produces a still and there is no image library here to make one. A `youtubeId` on the recording doc (set from a yt button on the /admin row, accepting any pasted YouTube URL form) swaps in YouTube's real per-video still, adds `embedUrl`, and renders the nocookie player so watch time lands on the channel instead of being split from it. `contentUrl` still points at `/w/{id}/play`, because the Daily mp4 is the authoritative full round. With no id the behavior is byte-for-byte the old one, which is the state of every recording today. The id is validated against `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{11}$` **at render, not only at write**, because it is interpolated into an iframe src and into structured data.
  - **Honest limit, stated so nobody re-litigates it later:** this makes /watch competitive for "watch debates online" style queries. It will not beat Open to Debate or the Jubilee results for the raw head term, because that half of the SERP is a video carousel and that is YouTube's game, not a page's. The remaining lever is inventory: recordings have to exist, and that needs debaters to opt in, not code.

- **App Check hard-enforced, and the anonymous per-IP ceiling raised behind it** (2026-08-18). Load-readiness pass ahead of an expected traffic spike. `APP_CHECK_REQUIRED` was `false` in the Netlify prod env, so the ~804KB of reCAPTCHA Enterprise shipped to every visitor was decorative: a plain `curl` with no token got a 200 from `/api/claude`. That left the anon per-IP counter in `claude.mjs` (5/min, 15/hour, 30/day) as the only thing between a script and the Anthropic bill, which is why the 2026-05-18 audit had to set it so tight. But an IP is not a person: at ~5 requests per free round that was **three anonymous visitors per address per hour**, so the fourth person on a school, campus, or CGNAT mobile carrier was told they had hit a "free-trial cap" they had never used. `realtime-session.mjs` hit the identical wall on 2026-07-28 and fixed it by moving NAMED users to per-UID metering; the anonymous lane has no such escape hatch, so the ceiling itself had to move. **Sequence, which mattered:** wire every calling surface first, verify tokens mint, then flip. **Two findings made that non-optional.** (1) `tts.mjs` and `realtime-session.mjs` call `checkAppCheck` at the top of the handler, *before* identifying the caller, so they need a token from signed-in users too — and `live-round.html` called both (plus transcribe and claude) with no App Check wired at all. Flipping against that tree would have killed every live round and all voice. `coach.html`, `live.html` and `high-school.html` were in the same state (high-school had no Firebase on the page whatsoever), and `pricing.html` loaded `app-check.js` without ever loading the App Check SDK, so its wiring was inert. (2) **The SDK injection race:** six client modules (`track`, `usage-banner`, `upgrade-cta`, `topbar`, `notifications`, `auth-modal`) each lazily inject `firebase-app-compat` when they find no firebase on the page. Whichever injection lands last owns `window.firebase` and takes the `appCheck` component with it, so a static `app-check-compat` tag registered against an earlier namespace simply vanishes. Measured on `/high-school`: two `app-compat` tags raced and `firebase.appCheck` came out undefined while every script tag looked correct. `app-check.js` now loads the App Check SDK itself at activation time, after the injectors settle. **Perf rider:** `activate()` is what pulls the 804KB bundle, so activation moved to first intent (pointer/key/touch) or the first gated fetch. A non-gated route attaches a token only if App Check is already up and never triggers activation, so public polls like `/api/watch-live` cannot drag it back onto page load. **New numbers:** `claude.mjs` 5/min → 30, 15/hour → 200, 30/day → 800; `realtime-session` anon 6/hour → 15, 10/day → 40 (voice rises less; it costs more per call, and named accounts keep the per-UID lane). Signed-in text callers are metered per uid and never touch these. **Verified on prod:** tokenless `curl` → 401 `APP_CHECK_MISSING`; landing, practice, voice-debate, high-school, coach, live-round and live all mint a valid 957-char token and pass every gated endpoint; 12 rapid anonymous calls that would have died at 6 now all return 200. **Tuning note:** there is no `UPSTASH_*` in the prod env, so both limiters run the in-memory fallback — counters are per function instance, making the effective ceiling both higher than the numbers suggest and unpredictable, since a cold instance starts from zero. Reverting enforcement is one env var plus a redeploy.
