# Conversation judge findings, 25 September 2026

A synthetic reproduction found one juror repeatedly treating a list of new questions at the end of a conversation as winning objections because the other person did not reply. That person had no remaining chance to reply. The other two jurors rejected the list, so this was an individual-judge failure even when the majority result remained correct.

The initial baseline used 12 cases in both timed and open conversation modes: 24 panels and 72 provider attempts. Twenty-three panels returned all three ballots; one response contained malformed JSON. It is recorded as a provider/output failure, not a wrong answer. The cases and expected behaviors were authored for this investigation and are not independently adjudicated gold labels.

On `closing-question-pileup` in open conversation, the xAI juror made the specific false-drop error in the initial run and both repeats (3/3). One explanation began: “Con wins because the final list of practical costs was never answered.” Another said the questions “remained standing after the round ended.” The fault was not which side label appeared; it was awarding substantive weight to unexplained questions and to a reply opportunity that never existed.

An experimental question-accounting block initially corrected that behavior in two candidate repeats. However, it failed again during integration and in one of two final-placement repeats. Putting the block earlier in the prompt also failed. The final placement was verified byte-for-byte against the original candidate, so placement alone does not explain the remaining inconsistency. A second candidate made the missing inferential link explicit: named logistical items are not established costs. That version also reproduced the failure. Neither candidate was deployed. Both are retained under `scripts/eval/candidates/` for reproducible comparison, and the published rubric, model pins and judging method remain unchanged.

Control outcomes were useful but do not erase those failures. Distinct supported objections and repeated objections with a real opportunity to answer continued to win. Two additional cases, written after the first candidate, used a remote-work topic. The late list of unsupported questions did not win, while a supported final-turn objection exposing an explicit contradiction still did. Those held-out outcomes were already correct in the baseline and stayed correct with the first candidate. These are narrow regression observations, not general accuracy measurements.

The next substantive experiment should test a change in the judging process or compare a replacement juror against this failing case and a broader set. More wording alone has not reliably corrected the affected juror. A replacement model would need live validation, cost comparison, wider regression checks and a new published season pin before release. This work does not swap models on one synthetic example.

Two parser defects were also fixed:

- Forty of the 72 initial responses lost part of their explanation at the old parser limit. Raw valid explanations ranged from 823 to 2,935 characters. A replay through the corrected parser preserved all 40 previously shortened explanations and changed no panel winner. The parser, panel normalization and split-ballot presentation now share a 12,000-character ceiling. The audit record still keeps its separate short excerpt.
- A missing, invalid or wrong-surface winner previously became a vote inferred from scores, including an automatic Pro vote when both absent scores defaulted to the same value. Such output now fails parsing and follows the existing missing-vote path. Deterministic tests verify that a third invalid response cannot turn a 1-1 panel into a manufactured majority. No valid baseline ballot had this missing-winner defect; this fix is supported by a direct parser reproduction, not a claim about the frequency of the defect in live rounds.

The reusable runner saves raw responses as well as parsed ballots, exact prompts, pinned configuration and unreviewed evidence checklists. It defaults to dry-run, requires `--live` for provider calls, and never writes rounds, ratings or audit records. Expected answers never enter the judge's prompt. Provider success is not counted as behavior accuracy.

A separate limitation remains: the clarification-only case contains no substantive policy debate, but the required Pro/Con output still produces an invented edge. This investigation did not change incomplete-round eligibility, no-winner policy, or round duration. The synthetic runner does not exercise the room's evidence gate. A real reported round and its ballot would be needed to verify the original incident end to end.
