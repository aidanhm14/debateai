QUESTION ACCOUNTING. Apply before crediting a question or calling a response missing.
1. Separate a question from an argument. A question may expose a necessary gap in the opponent's actual case, but it does not prove its own implied answer. "What about insurance?" supplies no reason that insurance costs rise. Do not invent that link for the asker. Credit only a specific, relevant gap or supported objection established by the exchange.
2. Count substantive objections, never question marks. Reworded questions about one premise are one challenge. An answer rejecting that shared premise can answer the whole group. Several distinct, supported objections are legitimate; do not dismiss them merely because there are several. Strategy rewards selecting and developing consequential issues, not exhausting the opponent's time.
3. Before writing "unanswered", locate a real opportunity to answer after the challenge was made, and check the entire record for an answer. A new challenge in the last turn, immediately before time expires or the capture ends, cannot become a conceded point because the other person was silent. It receives only the support actually supplied by its speaker; no additional weight for the missing reply. This does not erase a supported objection or excuse a dodge when there was a fair chance to respond.
4. Explain the inferential failure, not just a fallacy label. A conclusion repeated as its own support has no independent reason behind it. Naming a fallacy is not a rebuttal until the speaker identifies the faulty step. There is no fixed fallacy deduction and no automatic loss from a label.

UNPROVEN LOGISTICAL POSSIBILITIES. A list of nouns or questions is not a list of established costs. The judge must not supply the missing premise that a named item becomes worse under the proposal. The proposer does not acquire a new burden to solve every imaginable logistical issue merely because the opponent names it. Before calling such a question a necessary gap, identify the premise the proposer actually relied on and how the exchange shows that premise is missing or contradicted. If no speaker supplied that connection, do not invent a feasibility objection, describe the named items as concrete barriers, or make the absence of a reply the reason to vote against the proposal. A supported contradiction derived from the proposer's own words still counts, including in the last turn.
